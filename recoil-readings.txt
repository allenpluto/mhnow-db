Shot timings read off gameplay video, 2026-11-09
================================================

Method
------
Three screen recordings of a training dummy (480x1046, variable frame rate,
nominally 60fps but really about 26.5). Frame counts are therefore useless for
timing, so each recording was resampled to a constant 20fps and the "n HIT"
counter in the Total Damage panel was decoded frame by frame: the region
x=270..360, y=220..254 was differenced against the previous frame, and a change
of more than a dozen bright pixels taken as the counter moving. That times every
hit to 0.05s.

A hit is not a shot. Pierce and slicing rounds land several times per shot, so
shots are the first hit of each cluster; within a cluster the hits are 0.10s
apart, between shots they are 0.6-1.1s apart, so the two never run together.

Result
------
   Low        0.633s between shots   (3 gaps, one weapon)
   Average    0.865s                 (10 gaps, two weapons, agreeing to 0.003s)
   High       1.057s                 (7 gaps, two weapons, agreeing to 0.005s)
   Very high  not measured
   LARGE_2    not measured, and never seen as its own word on a weapon screen

Two different weapons agreeing to within 0.005s means these are the tiers
themselves rather than anything about the weapons.

Reload
------
All three recordings carried no handling skills and were fired as a full
circle through every magazine, so each gap from the last shot of one magazine
to the first shot of the next is a clean magazine switch.

   Rex Fusillade   into pierce/spread  Slow       3.65s
                   into normal         Normal     3.20s
   Dual Threat     into piercing       Very slow  4.00s
                   into normal         Fast       2.20s

Within one recording the player's own overhead is the same, so the difference
between two gaps is the difference between two tiers:

   Slow - Normal          0.45s over 1 tier  = 0.450s per tier
   Very slow - Fast       1.80s over 4 tiers = 0.450s per tier

One reading spanning a single tier and one spanning four, agreeing to the
millisecond. The ladder is evenly spaced at 0.45s.

Dual Threat also settles which magazine the tier belongs to: the long gap is
the one going INTO its very-slow pierce magazine, not out of it. So the reload
word on a magazine is the cost of switching to it.

The absolute level is the soft part. The two recordings disagree by about
0.55s on where the ladder sits, which is the player and not the game, so the
engine uses a least-squares fit across all four gaps:

   Fast 2.48   Normal 2.93   Slow 3.38   (large_2) 3.83   Very slow 4.28

These may run slightly long if either circle carried a moment's hesitation.

What this says about Recoil Down
--------------------------------
Timed by hand earlier on Anja Buster (flaming rounds, Average recoil):

   without the skill   8 shots in 5.90s over 7 gaps  = 0.843s
   with Recoil Down 2  6 shots in 3.23s over 5 gaps  = 0.646s

0.843 against the Average tier's 0.865, and 0.646 against the Low tier's 0.633
- so Recoil Down 2 is one whole tier, within the error of a stopwatch.

STILL OPEN: whether Lv1 is half a tier or a whole one. If the skill moves one
tier per level then Lv1 already does everything Lv2 does on an Average gun and
the higher levels are dead weight there; if it scales evenly, Lv1 is worth
about half. One timing of a full magazine at Recoil Down 1 on an
Average-recoil gun decides it - near 0.63s means per-tier, near 0.74s means
even scaling. The engine currently assumes even scaling.


What would be worth recording next
----------------------------------
1. LAGUNA BLITZ, four recordings, same full circle each time:
      no handling skills
      Recoil Down 1 + Reload Speed 1
      Recoil Down 2 + Reload Speed 2
      Recoil Down 3 + Reload Speed 3
   Its three magazines are normal x5 Average/Normal, piercing x4 High/Slow and
   sticky x4 Very high/Very slow - three recoil tiers and three reload tiers in
   one circle, all sharing the same player overhead. That one weapon gives the
   Very high recoil tier, the absolute level of the reload ladder, and both
   skill ladders at once. Sticky hits once per round, unlike slicing, so every
   shot is readable.

2. QUICKCASTER, one screenshot of the weapon screen, no recording needed.
   It is the only weapon carrying both an unknown tier on recoil (sticky) and
   on reload (pierce and normal), so the screen says in one look whether those
   show as High or Very high, and as Slow or Very slow. The data holds five
   recoil tiers and five reload tiers but the game has only ever shown four
   words for each, so two of them share a word and this says which.

3. Nothing else. Low, Average and High recoil are settled, and the reload
   spacing is settled.

A prediction worth checking against run 2 above: if Reload Speed also moves
one tier per level, it is worth about 0.45s out of a cycle of six to eight
seconds, which is nearer 6% a level than the 15% at Lv3 the engine currently
assumes for it - so the skill may be overrated in the suggester today.

Rules for the recordings
------------------------
   - no Spare Shot and no Evading Reload; both break the magazine count
   - keep firing straight through the switch, since the gap is the measurement
   - one full circle and then a few more shots, so the last magazine's own
     switch cost is visible too
   - keep the Total Damage panel on screen; it is the whole signal
