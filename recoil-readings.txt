Bowgun handling, measured from gameplay video, 2026-11-09
=========================================================

Method
------
Seven screen recordings of a training dummy (480x1046, variable frame rate,
nominally 60fps but really about 26.5). Frame counts are therefore useless for
timing, so each recording was resampled to a constant 20fps and the "n HIT"
counter in the Total Damage panel was decoded frame by frame: the region
x=270..360, y=220..254 was differenced against the previous frame, and a change
of more than a dozen bright pixels taken as the counter moving. That times every
hit to 0.05s.

A hit is not a shot. Pierce and sticky rounds land more than once, so shots are
the first hit of each cluster; within a cluster the hits are 0.10s apart, and
between shots 0.6 to 2.1s, so the two never run together.

Every recording used equipment carrying nothing that touches handling, except
where a level of Recoil Down and Reload Speed is named, and each was fired as a
full circle through every magazine without pausing.

The weapon screen shows the tier AFTER skills
---------------------------------------------
Four recordings are one weapon, LAGUNA BLITZ, fired the same way at Recoil
Down and Reload Speed 0, 1, 2 and 3, with a weapon-screen screenshot at each
level. Its three magazines are water x5, piercing water x4 and sticky water x4,
and the screenshots read:

   recoil           base        Lv1          Lv2          Lv3
     water          Average     Low          Low          Low
     piercing       High        High         Average      Low
     sticky         Very high   Very high    Very high    High

   reload           base        Lv1          Lv2          Lv3
     water          Normal      Fast         Fast         Fast
     piercing       Slow        Slow         Normal       Fast
     sticky         Very slow   Very slow    Very slow    Slow

THE CENTRAL FINDING: the timings agree with those words exactly.

   piercing at Lv1  reads High        fires 1.05s   same as at Lv0
   piercing at Lv2  reads Average     fires 0.87s   same as a native Average
   piercing at Lv3  reads Low         fires 0.60s   same as a native Low
   sticky   at Lv3  reads High        fires 1.08s   down from 2.05s

So the displayed tier is the whole story for timing. Nothing is hidden inside
a tier, and a handling skill is worth exactly the bands it moves you down -
which also means it is worth NOTHING when it moves you none.

Shot interval, by the tier on screen
------------------------------------
   Low         0.603s   18 gaps, 3 weapons
   Average     0.862s   17 gaps, 3 weapons
   High        1.063s   19 gaps, 4 weapons
   Very high   2.056s    9 gaps, 1 weapon

Very high is nearly double High, not one more even step. Weapons agree with
each other to within 0.005s on the tiers they share.

Reload gap, Laguna Blitz
------------------------
A bowgun reloads by switching magazines, so the gap from the last shot of one
to the first shot of the next is the reload. Only Laguna Blitz is used here,
because all four clips are one weapon fired one way, so whatever fixed cost
the switch carries is the same throughout.

   Fast        1.90s   1 reading
   Normal      2.85s   1 reading
   Slow        3.30s   3 readings, 3.40 / 3.20 / 3.30
   Very slow   4.83s   3 readings, 4.90 / 5.00 / 4.60

Rex Fusillade, a different weapon, puts Slow 0.45s above Normal; Laguna Blitz
puts it 0.45s above Normal too, to the millisecond. The absolute level does
carry a per-weapon offset, though - Rex sits about 0.35s above Laguna on both
tiers - so another weapon's reloads may run a little longer or shorter
together. The figures above are Laguna's.

Dual Threat settles which magazine the tier belongs to: its long gap is the
one going INTO its very-slow pierce magazine, not out of it. The word on a
magazine is the cost of switching to it.

Five values, four words
-----------------------
The data holds five recoil values and five reload values, but the weapon
screen has only ever shown four words for each. QUICKCASTER carries the two
that had never been seen, and shows RECOIL_LARGE_2 as "High" and
RELOAD_LARGE_2 as "Slow" - the same words as plain LARGE. So two values share
a band.

Within a band the skills still tell them apart, which is how the ladder below
is pinned. A skill that simply dropped one band per level cannot fit the
Laguna Blitz table: High would have fallen to Average at Lv1, and it did not.
What fits all twenty-four cells is a finer ladder on which the skill subtracts
its own level:

   step 0   SMALL         Low / Fast
   step 1   MEDIUM        Average / Normal
   step 2   LARGE_2       High / Slow
   step 3   LARGE         High / Slow
   step 4   -             Very high / Very slow
   step 5   -             Very high / Very slow
   step 6   EXTRA_LARGE   Very high / Very slow

LARGE_2 therefore sits at the BOTTOM of the High band and LARGE at the top,
despite the name. That is forced: Laguna's High magazine is LARGE and still
read High after one level, so there must be another step below it inside the
same band. Steps 4 and 5 are empty because no ammo in the data sits there.

PREDICTED AND CONFIRMED on a weapon the ladder was never fitted to. Dual
Threat at Recoil Down 3 and Reload Speed 3 should read Low on its normal
magazine, which is already at the floor, and on its pierce magazine should
fall from Average to Low on recoil - step 1 less 3, floored at 0 - and from
Very slow to Slow on reload, step 6 less 3 landing on step 3. The weapon
screen reads Low/Fast and Low/Slow. Both right.

The LARGE_2 row is the one part of this that is derived rather than watched -
no recording has ever had a LARGE_2 magazine with a handling skill on. It
predicts LARGE_2 falls to Average at Lv1 and Low at Lv2.

What this does to the two skills
--------------------------------
Both are now priced per weapon from its own magazines, not as a flat ladder.
The cycle is (capacity - 1) shot intervals plus one reload gap, weighted by
how much of the hunt each magazine is fired for.

   Laguna Blitz    Recoil Down    6.6 / 10.0 / 27.9 %
                   Reload Speed   5.9 /  8.4 / 20.5 %
   Anja Buster     Recoil Down   11.0 / 11.0 / 29.3 %
                   Reload Speed   7.6 / 10.9 / 19.3 %
   Dual Threat     Recoil Down   10.0 / 10.0 / 10.0 %
                   Reload Speed   0.0 /  0.0 /  8.2 %
   Rose Assault    Recoil Down    0.0 /  5.1 / 26.2 %
                   Reload Speed  12.0 / 15.7 / 25.8 %

Recoil Down 1 is worth nothing at all on Rose Assault, and Reload Speed 1 and
2 nothing at all on Dual Threat, because neither moves a band on the rounds
those weapons carry. Both skills jump sharply at Lv3 wherever a Very high or
Very slow magazine finally drops a band. The old flat 10/20/28 and 5/10/15
ladders could express none of this.

Styles
------
Two more recordings, one light bowgun and one heavy, each switching style
partway through via the dropdown at the top of the screen. A style changes the
firing itself, by far more than any skill does.

STEPPING SHOT replaces the shot with a dodge, so the cadence is the dodge's
rather than the ammo's. Anja Buster, one recording:

   sticky fire  natively Very high  2.056s   fired at 0.975s
   flaming      natively Average    0.862s   fired at about 1.00s

Two magazines three tiers apart landing within 0.03s of each other. The
interval is fixed, which makes the style an enormous gain on bad recoil, a
real loss on good recoil, and makes Recoil Down worth nothing under it. The
game's own tooltip for the style says "fixed-speed reloads", and the one
reload seen under it ran about 2.0s against a native Normal of 2.85 - one
reading, so not used.

CROUCHING SHOT accelerates while firing and carries extra rounds:

   burst of twelve   0.60 0.55 0.55 0.45 0.35 0.35 0.30 0.30 0.35 0.35 0.35
   burst of eight    0.30 throughout

Those are two magazines, not one firing past its capacity - pierce at 8 plus
4, and normal at 4 plus 4. The extra rounds are the game's own published rule:

   normal, piercing, spread   +4
   slicing, sticky            +2
   cluster                    +1
   wyvern                     +0

The cadence does NOT start from the ammo's own interval. Pierce is natively
Average at 0.862s and opened at 0.60s. It opens near 0.60 whatever the rounds
and falls to about 0.30, and the second burst running flat at 0.30 says the
acceleration survives a magazine change as long as firing continues. The ramp
in the engine comes from the twelve-shot burst, which is the one that clearly
started cold, so it understates continuous fire.

Recoil Down does NOT touch the crouching shot cadence. Worth checking rather
than assuming, and checked: the same weapon and the same twelve-round pierce
magazine with Recoil Down 3 went

   0.60 0.55 0.55 0.40 0.40 0.35 0.30 0.35 0.35 0.30 0.35

against 4.50s without the skill, and 4.50s with it - identical to the
hundredth. The skill was certainly working, since the weapon screen had moved
that magazine from Average to Low. Crouching shot simply does not care.

SLIDING RELOAD swaps the magazine on a dodge, and will do it on a magazine
that is not empty. Anja Buster, one shot then one slide, repeated - gaps
between consecutive shots:

   2.05  2.50  2.90  2.50  3.00  2.35     mean 2.52s

and, read against the magazine indicator, which magazine was fired makes no
difference: flaming at Average leads to 2.05 2.90 3.00 and sticky at Very
high to 2.50 2.50 2.35. The slide cancels the shot's own recovery. Taking one
shot out of the mean leaves about 1.65s for the slide itself, which is what
the engine uses.

GUARD RELOAD swaps the magazine without firing and loads only HALF the rounds
- visible directly, a hold-and-slide putting 4 of 8 pierce rounds up, and the
four shots that followed ran 0.80 0.90 0.85, the Average tier, and then
stopped. On an empty magazine it reloads on a single press, and Dual Threat
did that in 2.05s from last shot to next, against the 4.83s its pierce
magazine costs unaided. So guard reload is a Fast reload on a Very slow
magazine, bought with half the capacity.

Both styles explain mechanically, rather than by assertion, why neither wants
Reload Speed: the reload tier stops being on the critical path at all.

A CAUTION on everything timed here. Shots are read off the damage counter, so
a shot's time is when its damage lands. For intervals within a magazine that
is exact, because the offset cancels. For a magazine switch it does not: the
gap also holds whatever the player did between the two shots. The reload
figures above are therefore an upper bound on the game's own reload, and only
the differences between tiers are solid.

Still open
----------
   - The LARGE_2 row above is derived. One recording of a weapon carrying a
     LARGE_2 magazine at Recoil Down 1 would confirm or break it; Quickcaster
     is the obvious candidate, its sticky rounds being LARGE_2 recoil.
   - Steps 4 and 5 are unoccupied in the data, so EXTRA_LARGE sitting exactly
     at step 6 is the simplest fit rather than a proven one. Any value that
     takes three levels to leave the Very high band behaves identically, so
     this does not matter unless a skill reaches level 4 or more.
   - The reload figures carry a per-weapon offset of a few tenths. Only the
     differences between tiers are solid.
   - Sliding reload and guard reload have never been timed.
   - Crouching shot's magazine size is unknown; only its cadence is modelled.
   - Evading Reload is still a guess. The dodge is now measured at 0.975s,
     which is what an evade costs, so a reload replaced by an evade saves
     between 1.5s and 3.9s depending on the tier. If the skill replaced every
     reload outright it would be worth about 35% on a six-round magazine
     behind a Normal reload - far above the 15% assumed for it. What is not
     known is how much of a magazine one evade refills, and how often an evade
     is available, so no number is taken from this yet.
