The Switch Axe, move by move, and the first mechanical account of
why the Charge Blade out-damages it
=================================================================
Dummy, 2026-11-18, recording 17-26-37. Lumu Smasher 5: Switch Axe, 1949
attack, 0% affinity chosen deliberately so the damage is stable, no
element, and NO STYLE SELECTED. Stage boundaries timed by the player to
a hundredth of a second; damage and hit counts off the dummy panel,
which resets between every stage so each total stands alone.

HOW A MOVE IS TIMED, which turned out to matter more than expected
------------------------------------------------------------------
Each window runs from the first frame of the action to the frame the
character returns to a standing position - NOT from first damage to last
damage. The recovery frames do no damage but you cannot do anything else
during them, so a dps figure that excludes them describes a rate nobody
can sustain.

THAT IS NOT JUST PRINCIPLE, IT MEASURES BETTER. The same move was
recorded twice (S1 and S4). On full windows those two land 2.4% apart.
On first-damage-to-last-damage windows they land 8.6% apart, because
that convention depends on exactly where the final tick happened to fall
inside a recovery. The stricter definition is also the more repeatable
one, which is the sort of agreement worth noticing.

   repeatability of this method: 2.4%

THE FIVE STAGES
---------------
   stage  mode   move                   window    dmg  hits   dps  hits/s  /hit
   S1     axe    tap-and-hold           11.89s  10623   13    893   1.09    817
   S2     axe    tap tap tap             9.98s   8491    8    851   0.80   1061
   S3     sword  tap tap tap            14.14s  17533   27   1240   1.91    649
   S4     axe    tap-and-hold (repeat)   8.15s   7459    9    915   1.10    829
   S5     sword  charged + explosion     9.06s   9503   12   1049   1.32    792

   axe mean 886 dps. Axe attacks recharge the bar and are free forever;
   sword attacks drain it. S1/S2 and S4/S5 are consecutive, so the axe
   stages are also what pays for the sword stages.

EVERY MOVE IN A STAGE IS BALANCED TO THE SAME DPS
-------------------------------------------------
   axe    tap-and-hold   1.09 hits/s x  817 = 893
   axe    tap tap tap    0.80 hits/s x 1061 = 851        5% apart
   sword  tap tap tap    2.07 hits/s x  649 = 1342
   sword  charged hold   1.68 hits/s x  792 = 1327       1.1% apart

(the sword figures with the transform taken off - see below)

FOUR MOVES, TWO STAGES, AND INSIDE EACH STAGE THEY TIE. The faster move
always hits for proportionally less. At 2.4% repeatability the 1.1% gap
between the two sword moves is nothing at all and the 5% between the axe
moves is barely something. This looks like deliberate balancing.

WHICH MEANS THE CHOICE IS NOT ABOUT DAMAGE - EXCEPT THAT IT IS, ONCE
SKILLS ARE ON. Burst, Combo Master and Advanced Burst all count actions.
Two moves that tie on raw dps do not tie on a build that pays per hit,
and on this weapon the spread is 1.09 against 0.80 hits a second in axe
and 2.07 against 1.68 in sword. A hit-counting build should prefer the
faster move in both stages, for free.

THE CHARGED ATTACK IS NOT WORSE. ITS TRANSFORM IS SLOWER.
---------------------------------------------------------
On the stated windows S5 looks clearly worse than S3, 1049 against 1240.
Take the transform off each and they are identical:

   S3  transform ends 27.18, sword runs to 40.24   13.06s   1342 dps
   S5  transform ends 55.08, sword runs to 62.24    7.16s   1327 dps

The whole difference is the transform: 1.08s on S3 against 1.90s on S5.

*** THE FOLLOWING LEAD IS DEAD. See the transform section below. ***
S3 activates sword mode with a TAP and S5 with a HOLD, so the obvious
reading was that hold-activation costs about 0.8s more. Video 2 then
timed a TAP activation at 1.80s and a HOLD at 1.89s - the same. The
1.08s on S3 is simply an under-read, and the player has since explained
why: the transform's END HAS NO CLEAR ON-SCREEN SIGNAL, so where it
falls is a judgement that varies between attempts.

The explosion itself is real: the last second of S5 lands 4 hits for
4489, about 1122 each against 649 for a normal sword hit. It just does
not make the stage better than tapping, it makes it equal.

WHAT TRANSFORMING IS ACTUALLY WORTH
-----------------------------------
   sword, transform excluded      1335 dps     +51% over axe
   sword, transform included      1240 dps     +40% over axe

A cycle is axe to refill the bar, the transform, then sword until it
empties. S1 then S3 is exactly that cycle, start to finish:

   never transform, stay in axe       886 dps
   full axe -> sword -> axe cycle    1082 dps over 26.03s    +22%

TRANSFORMING IS WORTH TWENTY-TWO PERCENT. The sword stage is half again
as good but you only occupy half the cycle and you pay a transform to
get in. That is the number any Switch Axe skill has to be judged
against, and it reframes the player's own live fight where sword mode
was barely used as a much smaller mistake than it looked.

AGAINST THE CHARGE BLADE
------------------------
   Switch Axe    Lumu Smasher    1949 atk   cycle 1082 dps
   Charge Blade  Thanatos Force  2760 atk   cycle 2625 dps

   per 1000 attack    Switch Axe 555     Charge Blade 951     1.71x

Both weapons run the same shape - a slow state that banks a resource, a
fast state that spends it - and the spend states are not comparable:

   Switch Axe    axe  886 -> sword 1335        spend state  +51%
   Charge Blade  sword 913 -> Axe Boost 3244   spend state +255%

THE CHARGE BLADE'S SPEND STATE IS FIVE TIMES THE UPGRADE. It also holds
it far longer - 32 to 80 seconds in one block against a bar that empties
in 13 - which is why its live uptime was 80% in two windows against a
Switch Axe that transforms six or eight times a fight.

AND THE SKILLS AMPLIFY IT, which is the part that compounds. Axe Boost
lands 5.08 hits a second; the Switch Axe manages 0.80 to 2.07. On the
Burst 5 / Combo Master 3 / Advanced Burst 2 build both weapons were
wearing, that is a skill set which arms faster, breaks less and stays up
longer on the Charge Blade for reasons that have nothing to do with the
skills. The measured Burst uptimes already showed it: 0.448 on the
Switch Axe run where hits were not landing, 0.78 to 0.92 everywhere else.

   live base defences, Charge Blade against Switch Axe   ~1.9x
   dummy, mechanics alone, attack-normalised             1.71x

The dummy now accounts for most of a gap the engine still scores as
ZERO, and the remainder points the right way for the skill argument
above and for the group confounds in weapon-class-readings.

THE CAVEAT THAT MATTERS MOST
----------------------------
LUMU SMASHER HAS NO STYLE SELECTED. Thanatos Force was running Axe
Boost, and Axe Boost is worth +44% over the Charge Blade's own normal
axe mode. So this is a STYLELESS weapon measured against a STYLED one,
and it is not the clean class comparison it resembles. Strip Axe Boost
out and the Charge Blade's advantage per 1000 attack falls a long way.

Axe of Demons - the Switch Axe in every live fight in this project -
does carry a style, and nobody has recorded which one.

THE NEXT RECORDING THAT WOULD CHANGE ANYTHING is these same five stages
on a STYLED Switch Axe. If a style lifts it the way Axe Boost lifts the
Charge Blade, then most of the 1.71x is style rather than class, and the
axis the engine is missing is styleVariants() rather than weapon
category - which matters, because styleVariants() already exists and
already branches for Dual Blades.

ALSO OPEN
---------
   - whether the hold-activation transform really costs 0.8s more than
     the tap-activation. Two runs of S3 and S5 would settle it
   - the 22-spin Water Strike hammer mechanic, a third style that banks
     damage in a combo rather than a buff or a bar
   - whether any of this holds at other attack values. Everything here
     is one weapon at 1949 attack against one at 2760


=====================================================================
VIDEO 2: ELEMENT BURST COUNTER, WHICH CHANGES ALMOST NOTHING
=====================================================================
Same weapon, same dummy, recording 17-28-35, style Element Burst Counter
selected. The player's own read beforehand was "i dont feel significant
improve though", and that is what the numbers say.

   stage                          window    dmg  hits   dps  hits/s  /hit
   S1 axe  hold + slide up         5.80s   4538    4    782   0.69   1135
   S2 sword charged hold           9.12s   6994    7    767   0.77    999
   S3 axe  charge move (= S1)      5.07s   4538    4    895   0.79   1135
   S4 sword tap tap tap to empty  13.96s  16208   21   1161   1.50    772

THE 0% AFFINITY CHOICE PAID OFF HERE. S1 and S3 are the same move and
dealt EXACTLY the same damage - 4538 over 4 hits, with the same
685 / 1294 / 4538 progression. The move is deterministic, so every bit
of the dps difference between them is timing, and that difference is
14.4%.

   repeatability, video 2 (5-second moves):   14.4%
   repeatability, video 1 (8-12 second moves): 2.4%

SHORT MOVES ARE MUCH HARDER TO TIME PROPORTIONALLY, which is worth
knowing before anyone reads a 5% difference off a 5-second window.
Nothing under about 15% in this video is real.

AGAINST THE STYLELESS RUN
-------------------------
   axe moves       no style 893 / 851 / 915 (886)    EBC 782 / 895 (839)
   sword tap tap   no style 1240                     EBC 1161
   sword charged   no style 1049                     EBC  767

WHAT THE STYLE ACTUALLY BUYS is charge rate, not damage. The hold-and-
slide-up move filled about 75% of the bar in 5.80s, so a full bar costs
roughly 7.7s against the styleless run's 11.9s - about 35% faster. Put
that through the cycle:

   cycle, no style                        1082 dps over 26.03s
   cycle, Element Burst Counter (7.7s)    1046 dps over 21.69s
                                             -3.3%

MINUS THREE PERCENT, INSIDE A FOURTEEN PERCENT NOISE FLOOR. The style
is a wash. It reaches the sword stage faster and does slightly less
while it is there, and the two cancel.

DO NOT READ THE CHARGED-ATTACK ROW AS A STYLE EFFECT. S2 ran on a bar
the player had only charged to ~75%, where video 1's equivalent ran on
a full one, and the charged attack plausibly scales with the energy it
consumes. 767 against 1049 is a bar-level comparison wearing a style
comparison's clothes. The tap-tap-tap rows (1161 against 1240) are the
cleaner pair and they are 6% apart, which is nothing.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE CHARGE BLADE COMPARISON
------------------------------------------------
The big caveat on the 1.71x was that it set a STYLELESS Switch Axe
against a STYLED Charge Blade, Axe Boost being worth +44% over that
weapon's own normal axe mode.

STYLES ARE EVIDENTLY NOT ALL WORTH +44%. The first Switch Axe style
measured is worth nothing at all. So the caveat does not dissolve the
1.71x - it means Axe Boost may be an unusually strong style rather than
styles being a general equaliser, and the engine's blind spot could be
one specific style rather than a whole axis.

Two styles measured, one on each weapon, so this is a direction and not
a conclusion. The Switch Axe's third style is recorded and untimed.


=====================================================================
THE TRANSFORM, AND WHY THE SWORD STAGE IS A CONSTANT
=====================================================================
Three transforms timed in video 2, plus the two from video 1:

   v2  9.23 -> 11.18   1.95s   (early, outside the listed stages)
   v2 28.14 -> 30.03   1.89s   HOLD activation, S2
   v2 48.20 -> 50.00   1.80s   TAP  activation, S4
   v1 53.18 -> 55.08   1.90s   HOLD activation, S5
   v1 26.10 -> 27.18   1.08s   TAP  activation, S3   <- the outlier

   the four consistent readings: mean 1.88s, spread 8%

TAP AND HOLD ACTIVATION COST THE SAME, which retires the "hold costs
0.8s more" lead from the video 1 section above. The player supplies the
reason the readings scatter at all: THE TRANSFORM'S END HAS NO OBVIOUS
ON-SCREEN SIGNAL. Where it finishes is a judgement, and 1.08s is that
judgement going short once rather than a different move.

   so: the Switch Axe transform costs about 1.9 SECONDS, +/- 0.1s,
   and nothing the player does at the input changes it.

THE SWORD STAGE IS THE SAME EVERYWHERE
--------------------------------------
Taking each transform off its own stage:

   v1 S3  tap tap tap       no style    13.06s   17533   1342 dps
   v1 S5  charged, full bar no style     7.16s    9503   1327 dps
   v2 S4  tap tap tap       EBC         12.16s   16208   1333 dps
   v2 S2  charged, ~75% bar EBC          7.23s    6994    967 dps

THREE OF THE FOUR LAND WITHIN 1.1% OF EACH OTHER - 1342, 1327, 1333 -
across two different styles AND two different sword moves. On a 14%
noise floor that is not consistency, it is identity. Sword mode runs at
about 1334 dps on this weapon and essentially nothing changes it.

THE ONE THAT DOES NOT FIT IS THE ONE WITH LESS ENERGY. S2's charged
attack ran on a bar the player had filled to about 75% and returned 967
dps - 27% below the same move on a full bar, for a bar 25% less full.
THE CHARGED ATTACK APPEARS TO SCALE WITH THE ENERGY IT SPENDS, roughly
one for one. That is a real mechanic and it is the only thing measured
here that moves the sword stage's damage rate at all.

   (two samples, so the proportionality is a shape not a law - but the
   alternative, that Element Burst Counter weakens the charged attack
   by 27% while leaving tap-tap-tap untouched, is much less likely)

WHAT IS NOW SETTLED ABOUT THE SWITCH AXE
----------------------------------------
   axe stage      ~886 dps, two moves, balanced to each other
   transform      ~1.9s, unavoidable, same for tap and hold
   sword stage    ~1334 dps, flat across styles and sword moves,
                  scaling only with how full the bar was
   full cycle     ~1082 dps, +22% over never transforming
   style          Element Burst Counter: no measurable effect

A weapon with far fewer moving parts than the Charge Blade, and with no
state worth protecting: there is no 32-to-80 second buff to extend, no
expiry to race, and the one skill the engine prices highly for it -
Quick Work - accelerates a 1.9s action inside a cycle whose entire
payoff is 22%.

VIDEO 3: SOARING WYVERN BLADE, AND THE VERDICT ON SWITCH AXE STYLES
=====================================================================
Recording 17-30-26, same weapon and dummy, style Soaring Wyvern Blade.
The player's prediction beforehand: "my feeling is this style's dps is
even worse, tell me if i am wrong." Right in direction, overstated in
size.

   stage                                window    dmg  hits   dps  /hit
   S1 axe, tap-and-hold, 3 swings
      then a special slash that arms
      the axe shape behind the bar      14.79s  14540   20    983   727
   .. transform                          1.16s      -    -      -     -
   S2 sword, altered charge attack       3.84s   2682    4    698   671
   S3 sword, altered charge then the
      POWERFUL attack (which ends
      sword mode by itself)              6.20s   9149    4   1476  2287

INSIDE S3 ARE THE TWO BIGGEST HITS IN THE WHOLE SWITCH AXE DATASET:
5,361 and 2,622, against 649 for an ordinary sword hit. 8.3x and 4.0x.
The style's whole design is visible there - three swings in axe arm an
attack that lands eight times harder later.

THE THREE STYLES, ON ONE BASIS
------------------------------
Axe to fill the bar, transform, sword until it empties:

   no style                1082 dps over 26.03s
   Element Burst Counter   1047 dps over 21.66s      -3.2%
   Soaring Wyvern Blade    1015 dps over 25.99s      -6.2%

   full spread across all three:  6.6%

AND THE NOISE FLOOR IS 2.4% ON LONG WINDOWS AND 14% ON SHORT ONES. S2
here is 3.84 seconds. So -6.2% is real at best and unresolvable at
worst, and the honest statement is that SWITCH AXE STYLES DO NOT MATTER
- three of them land inside seven percent of each other, and the
styleless weapon is nominally the best of the three.

WHAT EACH STYLE ACTUALLY TRADES
-------------------------------
   style   axe dps   bar fills in   sword
   none      886       ~11.9s       1342 sustained
   EBC       839        ~7.7s       1333 sustained   (fastest fill)
   SWB       983       ~14.8s       698 then 1476    (best axe, best
                                                      single stage,
                                                      slowest fill)

Soaring Wyvern Blade has the BEST axe stage of the three and the best
single sword stage measured on this weapon - 1476, above the styleless
weapon's 1342 - and still finishes last, because the bar takes three
seconds longer to fill and the powerful attack ends sword mode as soon
as it lands.

A POSSIBLE OPTIMISATION, AND A QUESTION. S2 contributes 2,682 damage in
3.84 seconds at 698 dps, which is the worst stage in the dataset. Drop
it and go straight to the S3 sequence:

   14.79 axe + 1.16 transform + 6.20 powerful = 22.15s, 23,689 damage
   = 1,069 dps, which is level with the styleless weapon

Whether that is legal depends on whether the powerful attack needs a
charge attack ahead of it - S3 is described as "altered charge attack
FOLLOW with the powerful attack", so the one inside S3 may be the
prerequisite and S2 genuinely wasted. Worth one run to find out, and it
is the only route by which a Switch Axe style has looked like it might
matter.

WHY THIS MATTERS BEYOND THE SWITCH AXE
--------------------------------------
Three styles on one weapon, all within 6.6%. One style on the Charge
Blade - Axe Boost - is worth +44% over that weapon's own normal mode.

So the caveat hanging over the 1.71x Charge Blade advantage, that it
compared a STYLELESS Switch Axe against a STYLED Charge Blade, is now
answered as far as this weapon can answer it: NO SWITCH AXE STYLE WOULD
HAVE CLOSED THAT GAP. The best of the three is the one already measured.
Axe Boost is an outlier among styles, not an example of them.
