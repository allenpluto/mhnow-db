What a base defence actually is, and why the model's flat hunt is wrong
=======================================================================
Player account, 2026-11-17. None of this was recorded anywhere in the
project, which knew only that "a base defence runs about 150s".

THE SHAPE OF THE FIGHT
----------------------
The monster opens SHIELDED. All damage is reduced, the numbers on screen are
small, and they appear under a hive-like icon that stands for the shield.

Break the shield and "NOW IS YOUR CHANCE!" appears. The monster takes extra
damage and the numbers get much bigger. Then the shield comes back and the
whole thing repeats. THREE TIMES.

Each break states its own amplification, and they climb:

   first break    +25%
   second break   +50%
   third break    +100%

So a base defence is not one damage environment, it is at least six: three
shielded stretches at a reduction never yet measured, and three windows at
x1.25, x1.50 and x2.00.

THE DAMAGE COUNTER
------------------
The number shown with "Now is your chance" is the PLAYER'S OWN damage, not
the team's. That is worth more than it sounds: it was taken as settled
earlier in this project that a group hunt gives no way to measure one
player's damage, and that the health bar falls to four people's hits. It
does give one, on this fight type.

IT EXCLUDES EXPLOSIVE AND POISON DAMAGE. Those are added back at the scoring
stage instead. So on a blast or poison build the counter UNDERSTATES what you
did, and any future measurement read off it has to account for that.

THE FINAL SCORE, in order:
   damage score                      the screen pauses here
   + explosive and/or poison damage  the screen pauses again
   x event featured weapon boost     30% or 60% for the event's chosen
                                     weapon, nothing for the others

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE MODEL
-----------------------------
The engine prices one average hit across a flat hunt. Every uptime in it -
burstUptime, latentUptime, the Special Gauge timeline, Rising Tide's ramp -
is a fraction of hunt length with no notion of WHEN inside the hunt.

On a base defence, when is most of the question.

   A SKILL THAT RAMPS AND THEN HOLDS is worth more than modelled, because
   the windows come later in the fight and the amplification climbs. Burst,
   Combo Master, Latent Power and Rising Tide all ramp; all of them are
   therefore understated here, and the x2.00 window at the end is where a
   held skill pays most.

   A SKILL THAT IS UP FROM THE START AND FALLS OFF is worth less than
   modelled, for the same reason in reverse.

   NERGIGANTE AVIDITY GETS WORSE AGAIN. Its cost is the Specials you never
   fire - and a Special landing inside the x2.00 window is worth twice one
   landing while shielded. avidityCost() values them all the same, so the
   12 seconds in ASSUME.specialWorthSeconds is, on this fight type, an
   understatement of what is given up.

NONE OF IT IS MODELLED, and it should not be until the shielded reduction is
measured - without that the six phases cannot be weighted. What the engine
reports for a 150s hunt is an average over a fight whose real shape it does
not know, and that is a different kind of wrong from a mis-set constant.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT. One base defence recording, reading the damage numbers
in a shielded stretch against the same attack in a window. That gives the
reduction, and with the three amplifications already known from the screen,
the whole phase profile follows.

A NOTE ON WHICH RECORDINGS ARE WHICH. Not every live fight is a base defence:
an elder dragon boss fight has no shield phases at all, so it is a flat
environment and is useful for uptime work but not for any of the above.

that matters - as it stands, skill ramp and amplification are confounded and
the x3.47 cannot be attributed.


THE KINSECT, AND WHY THE PREVIOUS SECTION'S CONCLUSION WAS WRONG
================================================================
Player: on an Insect Glaive, once the bar below the Special button is fully
charged the KINSECT starts attacking alongside you, and it adds a great deal
of damage. Which glaive you carry decides the shape of it - some kinsects
attack far more often, some hit much harder. In video 3 it was up from about
50s to 1m50s, and Power Prolonger 2 is what stretched it to a full minute.

That window straddles the phases, and the previous section did not know it
existed. It attributed the 100% window's x3.47 to amplification compounding
with a skill ramp. THAT WAS WRONG, and the data contains the experiment that
shows it.

THE KINSECT EXPIRES MID-WINDOW, at about 110s, while the amplification stays
fixed at 100%. So the two can be separated with nothing else moving:

   104-112s   +154,158   19,270/s    kinsect up for 6 of those 8 seconds
   112-120s    +51,271    6,409/s    kinsect gone

Solving the mixed span gives 23,557/s with the kinsect against 6,409/s
without - A FACTOR OF 3.7, with amplification held constant. On this glaive
the kinsect is contributing roughly three quarters of the damage while it is
up, and none of it is modelled: the engine has no notion of a kinsect at all,
only the display names decoded in kinsect-readings.txt.

WHAT THAT DOES TO POWER PROLONGER, which the engine scores from a Dual Blades
measurement and applies to all seven weapon types at the same weight:

   POWER_PROLONGER = { 1: 0.030, 2: 0.068, 3: 0.120 }

On Dual Blades it extends Demon Mode and 6.8% at Lv2 is measured and right.
On an Insect Glaive it extends something worth 3.7x, which is a different
skill wearing the same name - exactly the mistake already caught once for
Dual Blades styles, where Power Prolonger is worth nothing on Feral because
the thing it lengthens should not be entered.

If the Dual Blades drain rule carries over (20% less drain per level), Lv2
giving 60s implies a 36s base, and across this 104s fight:

   without Power Prolonger   1.93x
   with Power Prolonger 2    2.54x     the skill is worth +32%

against the +6.8% the engine books. A FIVE-FOLD UNDERSTATEMENT if it holds.

NOT CHANGED IN THE ENGINE, and deliberately. That +32% rests on three
inferences stacked: a 3.7x from one pair of eight-second samples, the drain
rule carried over from a different weapon, and a base uptime inferred rather
than observed. Any one of them being out moves the answer a long way. What is
solid is the direction and the rough size - the current 6.8% is wrong for
this weapon and wrong low.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT, in order of value:
   - the kinsect multiplier, from a flat fight: damage rate with the kinsect
     up against the same fight once it drops. An elder dragon hunt has no
     shield phases, so it isolates this cleanly - video 2 may already hold it
   - the base kinsect uptime with no Power Prolonger at all, which turns the
     drain rule from an assumption into arithmetic
   - whether the multiplier differs by kinsect type, which the player says it
     does: "some will attack much more frequently, some will cause huge
     damage on each attack"


VALORWING, AND WHY THE KINSECT AND THE AIR ARE THE SAME FINDING
===============================================================
The kinsect on this Thanatos Glaive, from its own screen:

   Kinsect type: Flight       "Kinsects perform follow-up thrust attacks in
                              tandem with your AERIAL attacks after charging up"
   Performance type: Power    "Stamina decreased, making it more difficult to
                              enter a charged state, but attack power is high"
   Attack type: Blunt
   Kinsect bonus: Aerial      "Kinsects are boosted while in the air following
                              a Vaulting Dance"

THE KINSECT'S DAMAGE IS AERIAL-LINKED. Its follow-ups come in tandem with
aerial attacks, and the bonus boosts it further while airborne. So the 3.7x
measured in the previous section is not a flat addition sitting alongside the
player's damage - it lands disproportionately on the aerial attacks, and it
is only up for part of the fight.

That ties two separate findings into one. Skyward Striker multiplies aerial
damage; this kinsect ADDS aerial damage. They compound on the same attacks.

AND IT MEANS aerialShare = 0.50 IS CONSERVATIVE, for a third reason on top of
the two already recorded. The share was anchored on a 49% TIME share. Aerial
hits were already denser than ground ones; dead air drags the time figure
down and not the damage figure; and now, for the minute the kinsect is up,
every aerial attack carries a follow-up that a ground attack does not. The
aerial DAMAGE share on a Flight kinsect must sit well above the time share.

The Power performance type also explains the timing. Harder to charge means
the kinsect arrives late - 50s into video 3, 67s into video 2 - so on a
short hunt it may never arrive at all, and Power Prolonger decides how much
of the fight it survives into. On a 104s fight arriving at 50s, more than
half the hunt has no kinsect whatsoever.

WHERE THE CLEAN MEASUREMENT IS NOT. Video 2 was the hope: an elder dragon
fight has no shield phases, so a flat environment. But it is a Kushala Daora
with a two-segment monster health bar and NO per-player damage counter - that
counter belongs to the base defence UI. In a group hunt the health bar falls
to everyone's damage, so it cannot isolate one player.

So the kinsect multiplier has to come from a base defence after all, where
the counter exists, and the phases have to be worked around rather than
avoided. The 3.7x already derived does exactly that - it brackets the
kinsect's expiry inside ONE window, with amplification held constant - and
that remains the best available route. What it needs is more of the same:
the kinsect dropping inside a window, read at 1-second spacing instead of
eight.


THE 1-SECOND READ. BOTH EARLIER NUMBERS WERE WRONG
==================================================
The previous two sections asked for the same thing twice: the counter read at
1-second spacing instead of eight, with the kinsect dropping inside a window.
Both recordings now have it, and both of the headline figures derived from the
8-second samples do not survive it.

   16 Nov   Thanatos Glaive, VALOR 2            fight 20-124s   kinsect 50-110s
   17 Nov   Thanatos Glaive, SKYWARD STRIKER 2  fight 17-122s   kinsect 49-77s

Same weapon, same monster, same base defence, same build apart from that one
skill. 105 and 104 seconds of counter, every second, read off the screen.

IDLE SECONDS ARE EXCLUDED from every rate below. The counter not moving for a
second means no damage landed, and the 17 Nov fight has three long stretches
of it - 10s mid-window, 8s knocked out at 78s, 10s frozen at 105s, 34s dead
out of 105. Averaging those in measures the player's luck, not the build.

THE SHIELDED REDUCTION: ABOUT 0.6, NOT 1/6.4
--------------------------------------------
Each pair is a shielded stretch against the window next to it, with the stated
amplification divided out and the kinsect in the same state across the pair:

   17 Nov   shielded 1 / window +25%     1151 / 1868   0.62   no kinsect
   17 Nov   shielded 3 / window +100%    2340 / 3334   0.70   no kinsect
   16 Nov   shielded 1 / window +25%      963 / 2906   0.33   no kinsect
   16 Nov   shielded 2 / window +50%     5257 / 6797   0.77   kinsect up
   16 Nov   shielded 3 / window +100%    4496 / 8344   0.54   kinsect up
   17 Nov   shielded 2 / window +50%     4425 / 9607   0.46   kinsect up

Mean 0.57, range 0.33 to 0.77. THE SHIELD COSTS ABOUT 40% OF YOUR DAMAGE.

The earlier section put it at a FACTOR OF 6.4 and drew the conclusion that a
base defence "is overwhelmingly decided inside the three windows". That was
one two-second interval at the very start of a fight - cold skills, closing
the distance, the first hit not yet landed - and it was not a reading of the
shield at all. The shielded stretches are worth roughly two thirds of a
window, so they carry real damage and the fight is NOT decided in the windows
alone. The outlier in the table, 0.33, is that same opening stretch on the
other recording, which is consistent: the first seconds of a fight are slow
for reasons that have nothing to do with the shield.

THE KINSECT: ABOUT 1.8x, NOT 3.7x
---------------------------------
The 16 Nov fight contains the clean experiment. The kinsect expires at 110s,
INSIDE the +100% window, so amplification, phase and skill stack are all held
constant across the boundary and only the kinsect changes:

   102-110s   +133,504   16,688/s   kinsect up
   110-124s   +109,268    9,106/s   kinsect gone

   1.83x, from 22 seconds of 1-second samples either side of one event.

The earlier 3.7x came from the same two minutes of footage sampled every eight
seconds, which straddled the boundary and attributed part of the kinsect's own
decay to the span after it. At 1-second spacing the boundary is where the
player said it was and the figure halves.

The 17 Nov fight agrees loosely and cannot do better: its kinsect was cut off
early by a knockout at 77s, and the no-kinsect comparison has to reach across
that knockout and a freeze. It gives 2.9x. Take 1.8x as the measurement and
2.9x as the ceiling.

SO THE KINSECT ROUGHLY DOUBLES THE DAMAGE RATE while it is up, contributing
about 45% of the damage in that window rather than three quarters. Everything
the previous section said about Power Prolonger still points the same way, at
half the size: on a 104s fight, Lv2 is worth about +15% against the +6.8% the
engine books, not +32%. STILL NOT CHANGED IN THE ENGINE, and for the same
reason - the drain rule is carried over from Dual Blades and the base uptime
is inferred, so two of the three inputs remain assumptions.


VALOR 2 AGAINST SKYWARD STRIKER 2: WHY PLAYING CANNOT ANSWER IT
===============================================================
The two recordings above were made to settle exactly this, one skill swapped
and nothing else. They do not settle it, and the reason is worth recording
because it applies to every future A/B of this kind.

WHAT THE ENGINE SAYS, on the real build (Burst 5, Artful Dodger 3, Combo
Master 3, Power Prolonger 2, Advanced Burst 2, Weakness Exploit 1, and the
unpriced Partbreaker/Kushala Frostwind/Advanced Partbreaker):

      hunt    valor uptime     Skyward 2    Valor 2
        75s       71%             4954       5128    valor +3.4%
       105s       80%             4954       5200    valor +4.7%
       150s       86%             4954       5253    valor +5.7%
       225s       90%             4954       5295    valor +6.4%

A FIVE PERCENT DIFFERENCE. Now the measurements:

   whole fight, idle seconds removed     VALOR 6967/s     SKYWARD 5667/s
   final damage                          VALOR 578,228    SKYWARD 402,364

which looks like a 23% win for Valor until the fights are cut into pieces:

   base rate, early, no kinsect          VALOR 1935/s     SKYWARD 1509/s
   base rate, kinsect up                 VALOR 6027/s     SKYWARD 7016/s
   base rate, late, no kinsect           VALOR 4553/s     SKYWARD 2837/s

THE FIRST ROW IS THE WHOLE ARGUMENT. The monster's roar did not come until 45s
in the Valor fight, so for that entire early stretch VALOR WAS NOT ACTIVE -
the two builds were identical - and the Valor run is still 22% ahead. That gap
is the player, the monster's behaviour and the dice. It is ten times the size
of the effect being looked for, and it appears in the one window where the
effect cannot exist.

THE SECOND CONFOUND IS THE KINSECT, which is worth 1.83x and was up for 58% of
the Valor fight and 27% of the Skyward one, because a knockout at 77s ended it
early. That alone moves the whole-fight rate by more than either skill could.

AND THE PLAYER'S OWN THIRD RUN SETTLES THE POINT. On the SAME Skyward set,
unrecorded, they scored 1.2m, which at the event's x1.6 featured-weapon boost
is about 750,000 damage - against 402,364 on the recorded Skyward fight and
578,228 on the Valor one. TWO RUNS OF THE SAME BUILD BRACKET THE OTHER BUILD,
and the spread within one build is about 30% either way.

So: run-to-run variance is roughly +/-30%, the skills differ by 2%, and no
number of hunts recorded this way will separate them. Keep Valor 2 on the
engine's answer, and treat that answer as resting on the model rather than on
anything observed.

WHAT WOULD DECIDE IT is not another hunt. Skyward Striker 2 overtakes Valor 2
at an aerial DAMAGE share of 0.77; the engine carries 0.50, set from a
measured aerial TIME share. The kinsect is the only thing that could push it
that far, because Valorwing is a Flight kinsect with an Aerial Bonus - its
follow-ups come with aerial attacks - and it accounts for about 45% of the
damage while up. If Skyward Striker multiplies the kinsect's hits as well as
the player's, the aerial damage share on a kinsect-heavy fight lands near
0.68. STILL SHORT OF 0.77.

So on the figures as they stand Valor wins even on the assumption most
favourable to Skyward, and the swap is not close enough to be worth a test on
its own. Note how much of that rests on the roar timing: at the old and wrong
37.5s the tie point was 0.63, the favourable assumption cleared it, and the
test below was decisive. Correcting one constant turned a coin-flip into a
settled answer, which is a fair warning about how much weight these margins
can carry.

The test is still worth running for the aerial share itself, which feeds every
Insect Glaive build rather than this one swap: charge the kinsect, run a fixed
aerial combo on a dummy, read Total Damage; remove Skyward Striker, repeat.
Two numbers and no variance.


=====================================================================
2026-11-19: THE CYCLE IS FIXED, NOT THE OPEN WINDOW - AND TWO
RETRACTIONS THAT FOLLOW FROM GETTING THAT RIGHT
=====================================================================
The player asked a direct question: across the three shield-then-open
periods, is the PERIOD fixed or is the OPEN TIME fixed? Neither, as it
turns out. The CYCLE is.

HOW IT IS READ. The shield phase carries a countdown in a brown clock
face at the top left of the screen, and it vanishes the moment the
barrier breaks. That is a fixed-position UI element on its own disc, so
its presence is a clean binary. Two earlier attempts failed on the
damage panel instead: the first because the "Damage increased by X%"
text band also contains the bright blue shield badge, the second
because the open panel's own purple background is blue-dominant and so
is the monster's lightning. Validated against hand-checked frames -
shielded at 28s, open at 34s - which both earlier versions got wrong.

EIGHT FIGHTS, TWENTY-FOUR PHASES
--------------------------------
   SHIELD   mean 10.6s   sd 3.1   CV 29%    range  5.3 - 15.0
   OPEN     mean 24.4s   sd 2.6   CV 11%    range 20.0 - 28.8
   CYCLE    mean 35.0s   sd 1.4   CV  4%    range 31.8 - 37.3

   correlation between a shield phase and the open phase that
   follows it:   r = -0.89

THE PERIOD IS ABOUT 35 SECONDS AND THE SPLIT IS YOURS TO SET. Break the
barrier faster and the extra seconds go into the open window, nearly one
for one. The measurement's own resolution is about +/-1.4s per duration,
which is already most of the cycle's 1.4s sd - so "the cycle is fixed"
is as strong a statement as this data can make, and the open window's
larger spread is real rather than noise.

The player's account of the mechanic fits exactly: a 30s countdown runs,
landing attacks crack the shield, enough cracks break it early, and the
crack threshold rises each stage.

RETRACTION 1: OPEN TIME IS NOT FLAT
-----------------------------------
It was reported here that open time ran 91-96% across every fight and
was "the most stable quantity in this project and explains nothing".
That came from the broken classifier. Measured properly:

   Light Bowgun   527,060    80.6% open    shields 5.3 / 6.8 / 8.5
   Hammer         538,115    77.2%         shields 6.0 / 8.3 / 9.5
   Skullwhistle   426,091    76.6%         shields 6.8 / 7.5 / 10.0
   Switch Axe     227,211    68.9%         shields 7.5 / 9.8 / 14.8
   Hammer         333,215    67.9%         shields 5.8 / 14.5 / 13.5
   Hammer         372,901    66.3%         shields 10.8 / 13.8 / 11.0
   Charge Blade   471,759    65.1%         shields 11.3 / 12.8 / 12.5
   LBG solo       438,173    61.8%         shields 14.5 / 11.3 / 15.0

62% to 81%, a 1.3x spread, and it moves between fights. It does not
explain nothing; it is one of the things that varies.

RETRACTION 2: SOLO DOES NOT BREAK THE BARRIER JUST AS FAST
----------------------------------------------------------
Yesterday's note called the solo run "a clean negative result" showing
that three other hunters do not decide how much of the fight you spend
hitting. Wrong, from the same broken classifier. THE SOLO RUN HAS THE
LONGEST SHIELD PHASES OF ANY FIGHT ON RECORD - 14.5, 11.3 and 15.0s,
40.8s in total - and the least open time of any fight, 61.8%. Breaking
the barrier alone is slower, exactly as one would expect.

OPEN TIME IS A FEEDBACK AMPLIFIER, NOT AN INDEPENDENT VARIABLE
---------------------------------------------------------------
This is the part that matters for every comparison in this project.
Damage breaks the shield; breaking the shield buys open time; open time
is where damage is multiplied. So:

   more damage -> faster break -> more open seconds -> more damage

A build that is 20% better does not score 20% more. It breaks earlier,
collects a longer window, and scores more than 20% more. THE LIVE FIGHT
AMPLIFIES WHATEVER IT MEASURES, which makes it a worse instrument than
its noise alone suggests and makes every ratio taken off it an
overstatement of the underlying difference.

It also means open time cannot be used as a control variable. It is
downstream of the thing being measured.

WHAT A BETTER NORMALISATION LOOKS LIKE
--------------------------------------
Damage per open SECOND, which at least divides out the window length:

   Charge Blade   471,759 / 68.0s  =  6938
   Hammer         538,115 / 80.3s  =  6702
   LBG solo       438,173 / 65.8s  =  6660
   Light Bowgun   527,060 / 85.0s  =  6201
   Skullwhistle   426,091 / 79.3s  =  5373
   Hammer         372,901 / 69.6s  =  5358
   Hammer         333,215 / 71.3s  =  4674
   Switch Axe     227,211 / 70.6s  =  3218

Still crude - it ignores the damage dealt while shielded, which earlier
work here put at about 57% of open-phase damage - but it reverses one
conclusion. The CHARGE BLADE had the SECOND-LEAST open time of any
fight and still tops this table. Its advantage over the Switch Axe
widens from 1.9x on raw damage to 2.16x per open second, rather than
shrinking.

And the Hammer still spans 4674 to 6702 on one weapon, so none of this
rescues the live fight as a measuring instrument.


=====================================================================
PER-STAGE BASE DPS: DIVIDING OUT THE MULTIPLIER, AT THE PLAYER'S
SUGGESTION, AND IT CHANGES THE ANSWERS
=====================================================================
The player's objection to every dps figure in this project: the three
open stages run at DIFFERENT multipliers, so dividing total damage by
total time averages over a thing that varies. The right measure is per
stage: damage / open seconds / multiplier.

MULTIPLIERS CONFIRMED FROM THE PANEL, not assumed: stage 1 is "Damage
increased by 25%", stage 2 by 50%, stage 3 by 100%. The shield badge
also recolours - blue, orange, magenta - so the stage is readable even
when the text is not.

EIGHT FIGHTS, BASE DPS PER OPEN STAGE
-------------------------------------
                       open1  open2  open3    BASE    raw/s   open%
   Charge Blade 472k    3113   3962   4217    3785     4514    65%
   Hammer 538k          1865   4616   4699    3743     5174    77%
   LBG solo 438k        3229   3434   3470    3384     4114    62%
   Light Bowgun 527k    2062   3497   4216    3267     4996    81%
   Skullwhistle 426k    3173   2495   3118    2919     4117    77%
   Hammer 373k          2169   1789   3726    2638     3551    66%
   Hammer 333k          2459   2215   3221    2630     3173    68%
   Switch Axe 227k      2101   1155   2096    1754     2217    69%

   BASE = total base-equivalent damage / total open seconds

WHAT IT CHANGES
---------------
   Hammer 538k vs 333k        raw 1.62x  ->  base 1.42x
   LBG solo vs Skullwhistle   raw 1.03x  ->  base 1.16x
   Charge Blade vs Switch Axe raw 2.08x  ->  base 2.16x

THE HAMMER PAIR NARROWS BY A THIRD. Open time and the multiplier split
accounted for about a third of that 61% spread; 42% of it is real
performance. So the instrument was overstating, exactly as the feedback
loop above predicts.

THE TWO BEST-PLAYED RUNS SEPARATE. Raw damage put the Hunting Horn and
the solo Light Bowgun within 3% of each other - 426,091 and 438,173 -
and that looked like a coincidence worth remarking on. It was an
artefact: the horn had 77% open time against the solo run's 62%. On base
dps the solo run is 16% ahead. Open time was flattering the horn.

THE CHARGE BLADE'S LEAD GROWS. It had the second-LEAST open time of the
eight and still tops the table. Nothing about open time or multipliers
explains its advantage; if anything they were hiding it.

BASE DPS RISES THROUGH NEARLY EVERY FIGHT
-----------------------------------------
Six of the eight climb from stage 1 to stage 3, several steeply - the
Light Bowgun from 2062 to 4216, the Hammer from 1865 to 4699.

THAT IS NOT THE MULTIPLIER BEING WRONG. If the stated 1.25 / 1.5 / 2.0
were understated, making base flat would need multipliers near 1.25 /
2.5 / 4.1, which the panel plainly contradicts. The ramp is real, and
the obvious causes are the ones already measured here: Burst arms around
25 seconds in, Combo Master once at 10 to 24 seconds, and a player warms

THREE DUAL BLADES FIGHTS ON IDENTICAL GEAR, AND A COUNTER-EXAMPLE
=====================================================================
Set D unchanged - Thanatos Mauls 6, Kushala Crus legs, Burst 5 / Combo
Master 3 / Advanced Burst 2 / Skyward Striker 2 / Power Prolonger 2 -
played three times across two evenings.

   fight               damage    raw/s   open%   BASE   per-stage base
   21-25-57 (18 Nov)   320,784    3055    74%    2294   2227 2929 1818
   18-03-02 (19 Nov)   325,819    3103    71%    2687   3659 2561 1674
   18-08-34 (19 Nov)   420,743    4046    68%    3291   2627 3498 3832

   raw damage spread   31.2%
   BASE dps spread     43.4%

NORMALISING MADE THE SPREAD WORSE, WHICH CONTRADICTS WHAT WAS WRITTEN
HERE YESTERDAY. The Hammer pair narrowed from 61.5% to 42% once open
time and the multipliers were divided out, and that was reported as the
instrument overstating. These three go the other way: 31.2% raw becomes
43.4% normalised.

The reason is visible in the table. THE BEST FIGHT HAD THE LEAST OPEN
TIME - 68% against 74% for the worst - so it was doing more damage with
fewer seconds to do it in, and dividing by open seconds widens its lead
instead of narrowing it.

SO THE CORRECT STATEMENT IS NARROWER THAN THE ONE MADE YESTERDAY. Base
dps does not reduce the spread; it removes a confound. Whether the
number moves up or down depends on which way the confound happened to
be pointing, and with the feedback loop in play it can point either
way. The Hammer case was not a demonstration that normalising shrinks
noise. It was one draw.

WHAT IT DOES CONFIRM IS THE NOISE FLOOR, on a third weapon. Three runs,
one player, one set, nothing changed between them, and the fairest

DEAD TIME EXPLAINS MOST OF THE HUNTING HORN SPREAD
---------------------------------------------------
The player's own account of the bad fights: "the extremely lower one
might be the unlucky fight i got knocked out multiple times, or even
give up the fight in early stage, symptom is damage stop growing for
long period."

That is directly measurable - the base-defence counter never resets, so
a flat stretch IS a stretch with no damage landed. Sampling the counter
every three seconds through the best and worst fights:

   HH1  base 1909    dead 31s of 107s = 29%   four stretches, longest 10s
   HH5  base 3503    dead  3s of 106s =  3%   one stretch of 3s

HH1's flats sit at roughly 53-59s, 77-86s, 92-98s and 113-122s. THE
LAST ONE RUNS TO THE END OF THE FIGHT - ten seconds of nothing while
the x2.0 window was still open, which is the single most expensive
place in a base defence to be doing nothing.

CORRECTING EACH TO THE SECONDS IT WAS ACTUALLY FIGHTING:

   HH1   2670 dps live      HH5   3605 dps live
   the gap falls from 83% to 35%

So DEAD TIME ACCOUNTS FOR ABOUT 58% OF THE DIFFERENCE between the best
and worst Hunting Horn fight. The player's explanation is confirmed,
and the remaining 35% is ordinary play variation of the size seen
everywhere else.

AND IT IS NOT THE BUILD. The player confirms no set change across the
whole weekend - Hunting Horn, Bazelgeuse greaves, Set C throughout. So
the +53% step between 20 and 21 November is not gear. It is how many
knockdowns happened, which is a property of the fight rather than of
anything this engine could score.

THAT IS THE MOST USEFUL THING THE WHOLE BASE-DEFENCE EFFORT HAS
PRODUCED ABOUT ITS OWN NOISE. The instrument's spread is not mysterious
run-to-run variance; it is substantially one measurable thing, and that
thing can be measured and corrected for. A fight with its dead time
reported alongside its damage is worth far more than one without.

   quoted next to base dps
