Stance: Weakness Exploit against Sneak Attack
=============================================

The rule
--------
THEY ARE MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE. Weakness Exploit needs you on the monster's weak
point, Sneak Attack needs you behind it, and lock-on commits you to one. In the
player's words: when you use Sneak, Weakness Exploit scores 0; when you use
Weakness Exploit, Sneak scores 0.

The detail behind it: lock onto the tail and about 80% of hits land from
behind. The other 20% are the monster moving out from under the attack, landing
on a random spot - perhaps a tenth of those on a head weak point. 2% is not a
skill.

Status Sneak Attack carries the same condition - "from behind" - so it belongs
to the behind stance too and is worth nothing on the weak point.

What the engine used to do, and why it was wrong
------------------------------------------------
It treated the two as INDEPENDENT, so 70% x 80% = 56% of hits collected both.
The reasoning, recorded in commit 825b248, was that a weak point can be at the
back, so the conditions overlap rather than compete. That is now withdrawn.

It also left the two assumptions contradicting each other: 70% on the weak
point plus 80% from behind sums past 100, which could only be true if weak
points were usually at the back. As PER-STANCE rates they are consistent -
each one says how well you hold the position you chose - so the fix resolves
the contradiction rather than trading one error for another.

How it is modelled now
----------------------
Each stance is priced on its own and the better one wins:

   on the weak point   Weakness Exploit at weakPointUptime (70%), no Sneak
                       Attack, and Status Sneak Attack reverts to base proc
   from behind         Sneak Attack at behindRate (80%) and Status Sneak
                       Attack in full, no Weakness Exploit

A set carrying both now scores exactly the better of the two, never the sum -
checked as an invariant. score() reports which stance won, and both the page
and the command line state it, because a set now implies where to stand.

Checked: score() and the search's own scorer agree to within floating-point
noise on 2400 random sets across six weapons, status and ranged included.

What it changed
---------------
Less than expected, because behindRate at 80% already made Sneak Attack the
stronger of the two in most sets. Across a sample of five weapons at 75s and
150s, exactly one recommendation moved - and it is the one that prompted this:

   Bloom Snow Wail+, 150s hunt, Charge Blade, Ice, affinity -20%
      before   gems 1 x Weakness Exploit, 1 x Sneak Attack
      after    gems 1 x Latent Power,     1 x Sneak Attack

The winning SETS did not change, only the driftgems, since the sets were
already behind-stance. Search cost rose about 6% (3.4s to 3.6s on 9.3M sets)
for pricing the crit path twice.

A side finding, on negative affinity
------------------------------------
Measured on that weapon at -20% affinity, as marginal damage from one gem:

   +246  Meditation           not gemmable
   +242  Critical Ferocity    not gemmable - chase it on armour
   +195  Latent Power
   +193  Sneak Attack
   +142  Critical Eye
   +  0  Critical Boost
   +  0  Critical Element
   +  0  Weakness Exploit     under the stance rule, on a behind-stance set

CRITICAL BOOST AND CRITICAL ELEMENT ARE WORTH EXACTLY NOTHING on a weapon
whose final affinity stays negative - there are no crits to multiply. The
affinity POINT skills still pay, because they shrink the share of hits taking
the 75% penalty. That is the opposite of the usual advice and worth
remembering: on a negative-affinity weapon, chase affinity points or Critical
Ferocity, never the crit multipliers.

Before the stance rule, Weakness Exploit led that list at +199 and the
crossover against Critical Eye sat at a weak-point share of 50%: above it WEx
won, below it Critical Eye did. That crossover is what made the old 70%
assumption load-bearing, and it is why the question was worth settling.
