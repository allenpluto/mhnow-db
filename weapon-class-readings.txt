The engine has no weapon-class damage model, and it costs more than
every skill ever measured in this project
=========================================================================
Five Riftcharged Zinogre base defences recorded back to back on the evening
of 2026-11-17, within twenty minutes of each other, same player, same monster,
same fight type. Three builds, fully documented from per-piece screenshots.

HOW THE SCORE SCREEN WORKS, settled first because everything below rests on it
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
At the end of a base defence the result panel shows a number that COUNTS UP.
It starts at the player's raw damage and animates to the final score:

   final score = raw damage x 1.6      the event's +60% featured weapon boost

Confirmed exactly, twice:
   221,096 -> 353,754     217,076 -> 347,322     513,992 -> 822,387

and cross-checked against the in-fight counter on a third: the panel's base
figure was 287,426 and the counter read 286,249 two seconds before the fight
ended. So the base figure IS the counter, and the counter is what the score
is computed from.

THIS REPLACES READING THE COUNTER FRAME BY FRAME. A fight's raw damage is one
number on one frame of the result panel - the first frame, before the count-up
reaches the boosted figure. The per-second read is still needed for anything
about WHEN damage lands; it is no longer needed for how much.

THE FINDING
-----------
Axe of Demons and Thanatos Force are the same weapon on paper. Both are
grade 10, both 2658 attack at level 5, both -30% affinity, both no element,
both carry Partbreaker 1 and Advanced Partbreaker 1 and nothing else. The
engine scores them IDENTICALLY, 4822 and 4822, because it has no notion of
what a Switch Axe does differently from a Charge Blade:

   engine   Charge Blade vs Switch Axe, same armour, matched stats:   0.0%
   measured Charge Blade vs the BEST Switch Axe run:                 +65%
            Charge Blade vs the MEAN of the two Switch Axe runs:    +102%

A 65% floor on the gap, against a model that says there is no gap at all.

AND IT IS FAR OUTSIDE THE NOISE, which is the part that matters. Run-to-run
spread on one build is about +/-30% and is documented in base-defence-notes;
the two Switch Axe runs here differ by 37% from each other, which is that
spread again. 65% clears the top of it, and 102% clears it twice.

WHAT THIS DOES TO EVERYTHING ELSE IN THE PROJECT. Every skill question settled
or left open here moves the score by a few percent - Valor against Skyward
Striker by 2 to 5, guard reload by nothing, the whole Burst-versus-Valor
argument below by 7.6. The weapon CLASS is worth more than all of them put
together, and the engine cannot see it. A player asking this suggester which
armour to wear is being answered carefully inside a choice that was already
made badly.

NOT MODELLED, and it would be wrong to model it from this. What exists is one
pair of weapons and three fights. A class multiplier fitted to that would be
a guess wearing a measurement's clothes, and the gap is big enough that a
wrong multiplier would do real damage to the ranking.

WHAT WOULD MAKE IT MODELLABLE, in order:
   - the style customisation of Thanatos Force. Axe of Demons was at 10/20
     with 2 of 4 stat slots filled; Thanatos Force was never screenshotted.
     If it is at 20/20 with three attack slots that is +300 attack, worth
     maybe 10% - it does not explain 65%, but it has to come off the top
     before anything is fitted
   - the same armour on a third and fourth class. Two points cannot tell a
     class multiplier from a weapon-specific one
   - whether the gap holds at other attack values, which decides whether this
     is a multiplier on damage or a different attack rate

builds: Burst 5 / Combo Master 3 / Advanced Burst 2 / Power Prolonger 3
against Valor 4 / Raw Power 3 / Critical Ferocity 2 / Sneak Attack 1.

   engine, 104s multiplayer:  VALOR 5188   BURST 4822     valor +7.6%
   measured:                  VALOR 2067/s against BURST 2903 and 2126/s

The Valor run lands a whisker below the worse of the two Burst runs and 29%
below the better one. That is not a refutation - it is inside the same
+/-30% that defeated the last A/B - but it is the second complete build
comparison in a row where the engine's preferred build lost on the day.

Not acted on. Two paired fights cannot overturn a 7.6% model prediction, and
the unpriced skills on both sides (Artful Dodger, Partbreaker and Advanced
Partbreaker on the Burst side; Blast Attack, Chameleos Venomist, Headstrong
and the same two Partbreakers on the Valor side) are a real hole in the
comparison. What it does establish is that the weapon class above swamps the
question either way.

WHAT IS NOW KNOWN ABOUT WHY, AND WHAT IS NOT
--------------------------------------------
The dummy work the same afternoon (charge-blade-readings.txt) explains
the Charge Blade side: Axe Boost is an attack-RATE multiplier, 3.3x the
hit rate at 0.47x per hit, sustaining 3244 dmg/s against sword mode's
1152 - and it holds that state for 32s at a time, 80s with Power
Prolonger 3.

THAT IS HALF AN EXPLANATION AND IT SHOULD NOT BE MISTAKEN FOR THE WHOLE
ONE. It describes what the Charge Blade does. It says nothing about what
the Switch Axe fails to do, because the Switch Axe has never been put on
a dummy. Until it is, the 96% has a mechanism on one side only.

Thanatos Force is also 2760 attack against Axe of Demons' 2658 - +3.8%,
which was the stat-slot question the earlier note raised. It is now
answered and it does not matter: 3.8% against 96%.

STILL NOT MODELLED. A class multiplier fitted to two weapons would be a
guess wearing a measurement's clothes, and now that Axe Boost turns out
to be a STYLE choice rather than a property of the class, fitting one
would be wrong in a second way - the same Charge Blade on Normal style
loses 44% of its axe-mode output and the engine would have no way to
tell the two apart.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT, in order:
   - Axe of Demons on a dummy, same three runs. Twenty minutes, and the
     dummy's 1.4% noise means three runs settle what twenty hunts could
     not
   - a third and fourth class on the same armour
   - whether the gap holds at other attack values, which decides whether
     this is a multiplier on damage or a different attack rate

THE HAMMER ALONE SPANS 333,215 TO 538,115
-----------------------------------------
Three fights, same player, same weapon, inside about twenty-five
minutes. TWO OF THEM - 333,215 and 538,115 - ARE THE SAME ARMOUR SET,
recorded three minutes apart. That is +61.5%.

The weapon-class claim written here yesterday was that the worst Charge
Blade run beat the best Switch Axe run by 55.8%, across seven fights
with no overlap, and that this "clears the top" of the run-to-run
spread. IT DOES NOT. The spread is now demonstrably larger than the gap
it was supposed to clear: one weapon, one build, two fights, 61.5%.

So the honest position is:
   - the DIRECTION still looks real. Five Switch Axe runs all land at or
     below 303k; two Charge Blade runs both land at or above 472k. If
     the true spread were 61% in both, some Switch Axe run should have
     climbed into Charge Blade territory and none did
   - the MAGNITUDE is not established and two Charge Blade samples
     cannot establish it. 55.8% was measured against a noise floor that
     was itself mis-measured
   - anything that reasoned from the 55.8% should be re-read with that
     in mind

WHAT IT IS NOT, which took some work to rule out
------------------------------------------------
Base defence is not a flat fight - the monster is shielded part of the
time and open the rest, at +25%, +50% or +100%. The obvious suspect was
that the big runs simply got more open time. THEY DID NOT:

   open time, all nine fights measured frame by frame:  91% to 96%

That is as flat as anything in this project. 18-28-56 (538,115) was open
93% of the fight and 18-25-25 (333,215) was open 95%. The 61% gap is not
bought with seconds of vulnerability.

The next suspect is the MULTIPLIER - 100% against 25% is a 1.6x swing on
the same open second - and that is a GROUP property in a multiplayer
base defence, not a build property. Two attempts to read it failed: the
text-width classifier smears because the counter's own digits grow, and
a shielded-to-open transition counter returns 6 for the one fight whose
Hunt Report says 3. NEITHER IS PUBLISHED. The hypothesis is live and
unmeasured.

THE REAL CONCLUSION IS ABOUT METHOD
-----------------------------------
A base defence has now defeated four build comparisons and one class
comparison. The instrument is too noisy for the question, and the noise
is plausibly other players.

   base defence, same build, best vs worst pair:   +61.5%
   dummy, same weapon, two separate runs:           +1.4%

Forty times better. Everything that failed to settle on recorded hunts
should be re-asked on the dummy, and the dummy is where the Charge
Blade's mechanics actually got measured. No further live fights should
be recorded to answer a damage question until the dummy has been
exhausted.

WHAT THE LIVE FIGHTS ARE STILL GOOD FOR: anything about WHEN rather than
HOW MUCH. Skill banner timings, gauge uptimes and roar times all come
off these recordings cleanly, because they do not depend on how hard the
rest of the group was hitting.


THREE NAMED MECHANISMS FOR THAT 61%, FROM THE PLAYER
----------------------------------------------------
The note above left the variance as "plausibly other players". That was
vaguer than it needed to be. Three concrete causes, and all three are
things the engine cannot see:

1. A TEAMMATE WITH A HUNTING HORN BUFFS THE WHOLE PARTY'S ATTACK. Base
   defence is four-player, so a stranger's weapon choice is a multiplier
   on the player's own damage. The two Hammer fights 61% apart had
   COMPLETELY DIFFERENT TEAMMATES - simshadows / willisk2155 / XiaoTwita
   against Han / Klord / Willisk - so this is live, not hypothetical.
   It cannot be read off these recordings: the horn's buff icons are not
   visible in the HUD crop and teammate weapons are never shown.

2. CRITICAL FEROCITY, which is in the later two Hammer sets and not the
   first, makes damage less stable by construction. A crit skill widens
   the distribution of every hit, which is exactly what a 61% pair of
   runs looks like. Note the weapon is at -30% affinity, so what this
   skill does there is worth understanding before trusting any run on
   that set.

3. WATER STRIKE IS A VARIANCE ENGINE IN ITS OWN RIGHT. The Hammer's
   style spins up to about 22 times, damage rising with the spin count,
   and a tap mid-spin cancels into a heavy blow whose multiplier depends
   on how many spins were banked. So Hammer output is a function of
   UNINTERRUPTED TIME, not of time. A fight where the monster keeps
   breaking the spin pays a fraction of a fight where it does not, on
   identical gear and identical open time.

   THIS IS THE FIRST MECHANIC HERE THAT PUNISHES INTERRUPTION NON-
   LINEARLY, and it predicts that the Hammer should have a wider spread
   than the Charge Blade, which banks its state in a 32-to-80 second
   buff rather than in a 22-hit combo. The Hammer's three runs span
   61.5% and the Charge Blade's two span 8.9%. Two samples against
   three, so it is a consistency note and not evidence.

AND IT IS NOT A LATE SURGE. At the same elapsed time - 53.5 seconds into
each fight - the 538k run read 203,796 and the 333k run read 112,990.
They were already 80% apart. Whatever separates them was acting from the
start, which fits a party buff or a per-hit distribution and fits a
single lucky stretch much less well.

ON OPEN TIME. The player rates it among the most important factors and
that is surely right in general - the difference between +25% and +100%
is 1.6x on every second. What the measurement shows is that it did not
VARY here: 91% to 96% across all nine fights, with the 538k run open
less than the 333k run. So it is a strong lever that happened to be held
nearly constant, which makes it a poor explanation for this particular
pair and leaves the multiplier split - which is still unmeasured - as
the part of it that could still matter.
