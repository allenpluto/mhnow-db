The customisation track, read off fourteen weapon screens
=========================================================

The source publishes nothing about this. weaponLevels only carries levels 1
to 5 within a grade; the twenty-step customisation track is not in the data
at all, so it has to be measured like everything else.

How to read a weapon screen
---------------------------
The black figure at the top is the SUM of the base value and everything added
since, and the green figure in brackets is that addition. So

   base = black - green

and the result matches the database's own 10-5 figures exactly on all
fourteen weapons below, which is a useful check that both are right.

The green bracket holds TWO different things added together: the customisation
track, and the three stat slots. Despot's Blitz shows element +420, which is
120 from its track plus 300 from three slots of +100; Skullwhistle shows
affinity +30%, which is three slots of +10% and nothing else, since the track
never touches affinity.

The shape of the track
----------------------
Four stat steps, then a fifth step that selects a style, repeating four times
to level 20. So the total at the end is FOUR TIMES one block, and the blocks
are identical - Sinister Shadekeeper's step 6 is Element +4 and step 8 is
Element +6, the same values as steps 1 and 3.

   no element      all four steps are attack
   with element    element, attack, element, attack

Within a block the second occurrence of each stat is about one and a half
times the first.

THE TRACK DOES NOT DEPEND ON WEAPON TYPE. Four pairs prove it, each sharing
base stats across different types and carrying identical tracks:

   Sword & Shield and Dual Blades   1817/516   both E4 A10 E6 A15
   Dual Blades and Hammer           2658/0     both A8 A12 A14 A17
   Dual Blades and Long Sword       1363/1436  both E10 A6 E14 A10
   Dual Blades and Hammer           1878/516   both E5 A14 E8 A22

It is also not a fixed percentage of the base. Expressed that way the totals
run from 4.70% to 10.07% of base attack, with no obvious grouping.

AND IT IS NOT BASE ATTACK EITHER. Bazelhawk Rookslayer has base attack 2327
and Snow Sisters 1903 - 22% apart - and both take Attack +18 then +28. Two
weapons that differ by a fifth in the thing being scaled cannot be taking the
same step by coincidence, so the attack steps are not a function of base
attack at all.

The odd one out is Despot's Blitz, not Wyvern Lovers. A third weapon at
1463/1420, Squawkscythe, also blocks 20/20, so two of the three agree and
Despot's Blitz is 1.5x both of them. The user suspects Zinogre is treated as
a special monster. With it set aside the remaining thirteen are consistent
with each other, but still have no rule tying them to anything in the data.

Ruled out so far
----------------
   weapon type        four pairs share base stats across different types and
                      carry identical tracks
   base attack        Bazelhawk 2327 and Snow Sisters 1903 take the same
                      steps; Illusory Frilled at 1878 takes smaller ones than
                      Snow Sisters at 1903
   a fixed percentage of base attack            4.70% to 10.07%
   a fixed percentage of base attack + element  1.39% to 2.46%
   a fixed percentage of the 10-1 figures       1.41% to 3.02%
   a share of the growth from 10-1 to 10-5      0.071 to 0.152
   grade span         weapons spanning grades 5-10 take anything from 1.17%
                      to 1.98% of base attack
   the number after the weapon name             it counts position within a
                      name block - Girros Hammer 1-4 for grades 2-5, then
                      Malady's Fist 1-5 for grades 6-10 - so it carries no
                      information the grade does not

What the ratios DO look like is three loose bands - about 1.4-1.5%, 1.9-2.2%
and 2.4-2.5% of base attack plus element - but nothing in the data sorts the
weapons into them. Lagombi and Jyuratodus land in the top band and both start
at a low grade, which is suggestive, while Sinister and Somnacanth both start
at grade 4 and land in different bands, which kills it.

AND PAOLUMU SETTLES IT. Lumu weapons have base 2113 and no element, and take
392 at level 20. Black Diablos weapons have base 2658 and take 204. A weapon
weaker by a fifth draws nearly DOUBLE the compensation, which no scaling of
the base stats produces in either direction - Mizutsune at base 1363 takes
only 64. It reads as a number set by hand per monster to prop up the earlier
sets, and the search for a formula is closed.

A CORRECTION to what is written above. The four pairs that "share base stats
across different weapon types" are all two weapons of the SAME MONSTER:
Sinister Set, Black Diablos Set, Mizutsune Set and Somnacanth Set. So what
they show is narrower than first claimed - the track does not vary by weapon
type WITHIN a set - and that is exactly what a per-monster hand-set number
would look like.

So it is a table, not a formula
--------------------------------
The readings are held as a lookup in the suggester, and ticking "spend the
three customisation stat slots" fills the attack and element boxes from it -
a weapon customised far enough to have those slots is at the end of its
track. Unticking takes the figures back out, but only the ones it put in, so
a number typed by hand survives. A weapon that has not been read says so
rather than filling in a guess.

The lookup is keyed on SERIES AND BASE STATS, not on the weapon name. Since
the track does not vary by weapon type within a set, reading one weapon
covers every weapon of that monster built to the same stat line - which turns
twelve readings into 100 of the 748 weapons on record. It is deliberately not
keyed on base stats alone: Rathalos and Zinogre both make a 1463/1420 weapon
and their tracks are 80/80 and 120/120.

Extending it is now cheap: one weapon screen per monster, at any stat line
that monster makes. No rule will come out of it, but the coverage will.
