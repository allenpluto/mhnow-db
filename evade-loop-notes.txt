Evading Reload on stepping shot: MEASURED
=========================================
Nifl Mist 5, training dummy, 2026-11-12. Attack 1411, affinity 20%, Water
1508. Stepping Shot. Skills: Evading Reload 5, Slicing Ammo Boost 3, Attack
Boost 2, Recoil Down 2, Defense Boost 1, Evade Extender 1, Last Stand 1,
Artful Dodger 1, Peak Performance 1. No Spare Shot. The slicing loop runs from
12.45s to 40.05s of the recording: seven magazines, twenty-one shots.

Method: the three magazine icons in the centre of the HUD were read frame by
frame at a constant 20fps, each icon thresholded on its own. That times every
shot and every refill directly, with no inference. The hit counter was tried
first and is unusable on this recording - the panel is translucent and the
slicing effects wash it out - which is why the magazine icons were used
instead. They are a better signal anyway: they show reloads as well as shots.

WHAT WAS PREDICTED, AND WHAT HAPPENED
-------------------------------------
Predicted here before the recording: that Evading Reload would top the
magazine up as it drained, that the magazine would never run dry, and that the
gun would fire one round every 0.975s indefinitely - 1.026 rounds/s,
independent of magazine size. A threshold was derived, p x cap >= 1.

THAT IS WRONG, and the recording kills it outright.

IT ONLY EVER FIRES ON AN EMPTY MAGAZINE. All fifteen refill events across
seven magazines began at zero rounds. Not once in roughly fourteen
opportunities did a dodge top up a partial magazine. The skill does not
stretch a magazine; it shortens the wait at the end of one. So there is no
self-sustaining loop, the p x cap >= 1 threshold is the wrong model, and
Spare Shot is not the threshold skill it was argued to be.

-----------

That is an independent confirmation of stepping shot's fixed cadence, measured

That is the first absolute damage-per-second figure in this project for any

WHAT THE ENGINE DOES NOW
------------------------
Evading Reload was a flat guess of 4/7/10/13/15% of damage with a comment
admitting it. It is now worked out per weapon by handlingTable(w, 'evade'),
the same way Recoil Down, Reload Speed and Spare Shot are: with chance p the
magazine comes up in 2.05s instead of what the cycle costs, and never slower,
so a magazine already faster than 2.05s gains nothing from it.

   EVADE_RELOAD_FAST   = 2.05s                      measured
   EVADE_RELOAD_CHANCE = .18 .31 .44 .58 .67        Lv5 measured at 4 of 6,
                                                    lower levels keep the old
                                                    shape rescaled to meet it

Checked against the recording: the model gives 0.680 r/s on this magazine
against 0.703 measured, 3.3% low - the residue is the 0.957s measured interval
against the 0.975s the engine carries.

Applied ONLY under stepping shot, which is where it was measured. The skill
fires on a dodge, and under stepping shot the dodge is the shot, so the
trigger is free and constant. Off that style a dodge is an action spent
instead of attacking, and this engine has no way to price that, so the old
flat guess still stands there and is still marked as a guess.

A COINCIDENCE WORTH NOT BEING FOOLED BY: averaged over Nifl Mist's three
magazines the new figure for Lv5 is 15.5%, against the old flat guess of 15%.
The guess was accidentally right in the average and wrong in the detail - the
gain is concentrated almost entirely on the slow slicing magazine and is worth
far less on the fast normal one.

WHAT STILL HOLDS FROM BEFORE
----------------------------
The ammo-share point is untouched and still a real gap. Parked on slicing,
Slicing Ammo Boost is worth its full +100% at Lv3, where the engine books 1/n
of it - a third on this three-magazine weapon. That is x1.5 on the damage and
is not in the ranking.

And stepping shot itself is confirmed twice over: a fixed 0.957s between shots
on a magazine whose native interval is 2.056s.

STILL OPEN
----------
   - Evading Reload at Lv1-4. Only Lv5 was measured; the ladder below it is
     the old shape rescaled, which is a guess with a measurement at one end.
   - Whether the 2.05s quick reload is a fixed floor or this weapon's own
     number. Guard reload measured 2.05s on a different weapon, which hints at
     a floor, but two weapons is not a ladder.
   - Whether "only on empty" is the skill's rule or an artefact of a 3-round
     magazine emptying faster than the chance could land. A recording on a
     5-round magazine would separate those: fourteen opportunities missed is
     suggestive, but a longer magazine would make it conclusive.


LIVE HUNTS: DOES ANY OF IT SURVIVE A REAL MONSTER?
==================================================
Frost Blitz 5 (attack 1483, affinity 0%, Ice 1448; Freeze 5 / Spread Ice 4 /
Slicing Ice 3), four recordings of 4-player Brachydios hunts, 2026-11-12.
Same skills as the dummy run bar one: Evading Reload 5, Slicing Ammo Boost 3,
Attack Boost 2, Recoil Down 2, Divine Blessing 2, Defense Boost 1, Evade
Extender 1, Last Stand 1, Peak Performance 1. Player's own account of the
build: open on freeze, switch through spread to slicing, and from then on
never reload by hand - only slide to trigger Evading Reload.

READING THEM WAS THE HARD PART, and two approaches failed first:
   - The hit counter and the Total Damage panel do not exist in a group hunt,
     and the monster's health bar falls to four players' damage, so neither
     can time one player's shots.
   - The audio track is SILENT in all five recordings, so gunshot onsets -
     which would have been immune to all of this - are not available.
   - Reading the magazine icons by colour fails: they are pale blue-white over
     a scene that swings pink to dark to orange. By darkness alone it fails
     too, because a dark scene makes every box look like an icon.

What works: an icon is a DARK OUTLINE around a BRIGHT BODY. A loaded bullet
puts its outline at row 5 of the box with a body over 130 luminance; an empty
slot puts its outline at row 12 or below; dark scenery has an outline at row 0
and a dark body. And because the indicator is CENTRED, a 5-round freeze
magazine and a 4-round spread magazine sit at different pixels from the
3-round slicing one, so guard boxes where only the wider layouts reach mark
those frames and drop them. Finally, only a level that HOLDS for 0.3s counts,
so a single bad frame can never invent a shot.

POOLED RESULT, 22 cycles over four hunts

   WITHIN a magazine, shot to shot (27 clean gaps)
      mean 0.926s, median 0.900s
      against 0.957s on the dummy and 0.975s on the original recording

   THE CADENCE SURVIVES. Three weapons, three recordings, a dummy and a live
   monster, native intervals differing by more than two to one: 0.975, 0.957,
   0.926. Stepping shot's interval is the dodge's, not the ammo's, and that is
   now about as well established as anything in this project. DODGE_SECONDS is
   0.95 as the pooled figure.

   EMPTY to the next shot (22)
      14 uninterrupted ones   mean 2.400s
      8 interrupted ones      4.5 5.2 5.3 6.0 6.3 6.9 9.3 19.7s

   THE EVADING RELOAD MODEL SURVIVES TOO, and this is the real test, because
   the model was fitted on Nifl Mist and Frost Blitz is a different weapon:
      engine predicts   0.67 x 2.05 + 0.33 x 3.30 = 2.462s
      live measured                                 2.400s   2.5% out
      dummy measured                                2.367s   4% out

   CYCLE, three rounds (22)
      uninterrupted only, 15 of 22   4.790s = 0.626 rounds/s
      ALL cycles                     7.300s = 0.411 rounds/s
      dummy                          4.267s = 0.703 rounds/s

WHAT THE LIVE HUNTS ADD THAT THE DUMMY CANNOT
----------------------------------------------
Interruption, and it is the biggest single number here. Seven of twenty-two
cycles were interrupted - the player dodging an attack rather than to shoot,
repositioning, or losing the line - and 34% of all cycle time went to it.
Even the uninterrupted live cycles run 12% longer than the dummy's.

So a live hunt realises about 58% of the dummy's throughput on this build, or
89% if you only count the cycles that were not interrupted. That gap is not a
property of the weapon, the style or the skill; it is the monster. Nothing in
this engine prices it, and nothing in it should - but it is the right thing to
remember when a dummy figure like 4874 damage per second is quoted.

CAVEAT ON THE LIVE NUMBERS. The icons are 10 pixels wide in a 480x1046
recording and are regularly covered by floating damage numbers. The plateau
rule makes a false shot very unlikely but a MISSED one is still possible, so
the live shot counts are a floor and the cycle figures could be slightly slow.
The within-magazine interval is the most reliable of them, since it only uses
consecutive clean single-round drops; the interruption share is the least.


RELOAD SPEED IS WORTH NOTHING ON THIS BUILD, AND SO IS RECOIL DOWN
==================================================================
Two exclusions, one already modelled and one that was not.

RECOIL DOWN was already correct. Stepping shot fires at a fixed interval
whatever the recoil, so the skill has nothing to shorten. The engine has held
that since the style was first measured.

RELOAD SPEED WAS NOT. Evading Reload puts the magazine up by a different
route, so the reload ladder that Reload Speed shortens is not on the path at
all. The engine was pricing the two as independent skills, which let a set
collect both on the same reloads.

The open question was whether an ordinary reload ever still completes. At Lv5
Evading Reload fires about two times in three, so a third of reloads might
have been ordinary ones, and Reload Speed would then be worth a third of its
usual value rather than nothing.

IT IS NOTHING, and the dummy recording already answered it. The seven refills
came 0.70 0.75 0.80 1.80 1.75 0.75 0.75 seconds after the magazine emptied,
against the 3.30s that magazine's Slow reload costs. EVERY ONE IS FAR UNDER
IT. No standing reload ever finished. The 1.75-1.80 pair is a second dodge
before the skill fired, not a reload completing - which also means the "two
times in three" is a chance per dodge, not a chance of avoiding an otherwise
ordinary reload. Hold the slide and the ordinary reload never happens.

So a stepping-shot build carrying Evading Reload wants NEITHER handling
skill, and the engine now says so in both scoring paths:

   Frost Blitz, stepping shot, Slicing Ammo Boost 3
      bare                                      utility   0.00%
      + Reload Speed 3                                   27.66%
      + Evading Reload 5                                 14.58%
      + Evading Reload 5 AND Reload Speed 3              14.58%   no stacking
      + Evading Reload 5 AND Recoil Down 3               14.58%   no stacking

Note the first two lines: Reload Speed alone is worth MORE than Evading
Reload alone on this weapon, 27.66% against 14.58%. The build is not choosing
the stronger skill, it is choosing the one that fits the style - and it is
right to, because Reload Speed's 27.66% assumes standing still to reload,
which is the thing stepping shot exists to avoid.

Checked: score() and the search's own scorer agree to the last digit on 4400
random sets across six weapons and every style variant.


WHY THE PARKED BUILD NEEDS EVADING RELOAD, NOT JUST PREFERS IT
==============================================================
The suggester first offered the parked-on-slicing build with RELOAD SPEED 3
and no Evading Reload at all, which scored better than the real build. That
is impossible in the game, and the reason is one already written down in
recoil-readings.txt as the basis of the entire reload ladder:

   A BOWGUN RELOADS BY SWITCHING MAGAZINES.

So an ordinary reload does not put the slicing magazine back up - it walks
you to the next one. Reload Speed makes you cycle through all three magazines
FASTER; it cannot keep you on one. Only Evading Reload refills the magazine
you are holding. The two are not competing ways to do the same job, which is
how the engine was treating them.

CONFIRMED ON THE DUMMY RUN. Across seven reloads in 29 seconds the 5-round
magazine never appeared - 578 of 580 frames showed the 3-slot layout, and the
two that did not are isolated single frames of 0.05s, reader noise rather
than a magazine in use. A reload that switched would have brought the 5-round
magazine up every third time. It never came.

So the parked variant now REQUIRES Evading Reload: a set without it scores
nothing there, and the even-use variant is what covers that set. Both scoring
paths agree on 6800 random sets over 17 variants, 1957 of which correctly
scored nothing.

What it does to the ranking on Frost Blitz at 75s:

   stepping shot, even use                      9670
   stepping shot, parked - the free ride       13804   NOT REACHABLE
   stepping shot, parked - needing the skill   11895
   sliding reload, around normal ammo          14368

The 13804 was the engine getting the slicing ammo share without paying for
the skill that makes it possible. 11895 is the honest figure, and the build
it names carries Evading Reload 2 with Slicing Ammo Boost 3 and no handling
skill at all - which is the build being played, allowing that the engine
prefers to spend the remaining armour on damage rather than on Evading
Reload 4 and 5.

THE REMAINING GAP TO SLIDING RELOAD, 11895 against 14368, is the one this
engine cannot close by measurement, because it is the same blind spot as
everywhere else here: sliding reload and stepping shot both exist to let you
keep moving, and nothing in this model knows what being hit costs. The live
hunts put a number on the half of it that IS visible - 34% of cycle time lost
to interruption - but that is the cost of the monster, not the benefit of the
style.


THE WAIT IS GEOMETRIC, NOT A COIN FLIP - CORRECTED MODEL
========================================================
The first model said the reload costs p x 2.05s + (1-p) x ladder. That fitted
the one weapon it was built on and was WRONG IN STRUCTURE.

The skill's own rule is a chance per dodge of refilling the magazine outright
- 70% at Lv5. A failed roll does not fall back to an ordinary reload; you
simply dodge again and roll again. So:

   expected dodges until it fires  = 1 / p
   expected gap, last shot to next = DODGE x (1 + 1/p)

capped at the ladder, since a magazine that comes up faster by hand would be
reloaded by hand. THE RELOAD TIER DROPS OUT ENTIRELY once the skill is
working, which the old shape could not express - it kept letting the ladder
back in, and it had no way to say "try again".

THE 70% CHECKS OUT against the refill delays, independently of the skill
text. Seven magazines: five refilled on the first dodge, two on the second,
none on a third. At p = 0.70 expect 4.9, 1.5 and 0.6. Maximum likelihood on
this run is 7 successes in 9 dodges, 78%, and 70% sits well inside that.

   model, Lv5                  2.307s
   dummy, Nifl Mist, 6 gaps    2.367s     2.5% out
   live, Frost Blitz, 14 gaps  2.400s     3.9% out

   model cycle   4.207s = 0.713 rounds/s
   dummy         4.267s = 0.703 rounds/s     1.4% out, against 3.3% before

A THRESHOLD FALLS OUT OF IT, which the old shape hid completely:

   Lv    p      raw wait    on a Slow 3.30s magazine
    1   0.19     5.95s        no gain, the ladder is quicker
    2   0.33     3.83s        no gain
    3   0.47     2.97s
    4   0.61     2.51s
    5   0.70     2.31s

BELOW LV3 THE SKILL DOES NOTHING on a Slow magazine - and more than nothing
is at stake, because the parked build is not a choice made once. It holds
only while waiting beats reloading by hand, since an ordinary reload moves
you off the magazine. So the parked variant now requires the lowest level
that actually beats the ladder for that weapon's magazine, not merely Lv1.

Before this, the search took Evading Reload 2 - the cheapest level that
satisfied a "carries the skill" test - and got the parked ammo share for a
skill that was doing nothing. It now takes Evading Reload 4 and pays for it:

   Frost Blitz, 75s, parked      score 11895 -> 10808
   and the set is Slicing Ammo Boost 3 with Evading Reload 4, no handling
   skill at all, which is the build being played.


THE REAL LADDER: IT IS TWO NUMBERS, NOT ONE
===========================================
From the skill's own text, all five levels:

   Lv1   30% to load 1      Lv4   70% to load 2
   Lv2   50% to load 1      Lv5   70% to load 3
   Lv3   50% to load 2

A CHANCE AND AN AMOUNT, climbing alternately. Every model here before this
assumed a full refill at every level, which made Lv1-3 far better than they
are and Lv5 better than it is on a small magazine.

THE ROUNDS ARE CAPPED BY THE MAGAZINE, and that is where it bites. On a
2-round slicing magazine Lv5's third round goes nowhere, so LV4 AND LV5 ARE
THE SAME SKILL.

The cycle, from empty: dodge until a roll lands, 1/p dodges on average, then
fire what it gave you, one round per dodge. So

   rounds per second = L / ((1/p + L) x DODGE),  L = min(load, capacity)

and the magazine's own reload never enters it. Never worse than reloading by
hand, because a player whose magazine comes up faster by hand would do that.

   3-round slicing, Slow 3.30s        2-round slicing, Normal 2.85s
      by hand           0.577            by hand           0.526
      Lv1 30%x1         0.243  worse     Lv1 30%x1         0.243  worse
      Lv2 50%x1         0.351  worse     Lv2 50%x1         0.351  worse
      Lv3 50%x2         0.526  worse     Lv3 50%x2         0.526  exactly ties
      Lv4 70%x2         0.614            Lv4 70%x2         0.614
      Lv5 70%x3         0.713            Lv5 70%x3 -> 2    0.614  SAME AS LV4

Lv5 at 0.713 r/s against the dummy's measured 0.703 - 1.4% out, and the
measurement is untouched by this change because on a 3-round magazine Lv5
loads the full magazine, which is what the old model assumed.

Through the engine, as the utility figure on the parked variant:

   Rose Assault  slicing 2:  Lv1-3 0.0%   Lv4 16.0%   Lv5 16.4%
   Frost Blitz   slicing 3:  Lv1-3 0.0%   Lv4  6.2%   Lv5 22.8%

Rose Assault's Lv4 and Lv5 differ by 0.4%, not 0: that residue is the 4% of
shots spent on its pierce magazine, which holds 4 and so can use Lv5's third
round. On the slicing magazine itself they are identical, as the text says.

Frost Blitz is the opposite shape and worth noticing: Lv4 is barely worth
anything there (6.2%) while Lv5 is worth 22.8%, because the step from 2
rounds to 3 is the step from two thirds of that magazine to all of it. THE
SKILL IS NOT SMOOTH IN ITS LEVELS - what it is worth depends on where the
load amount falls against the magazine size, so it must be priced per weapon
and cannot be a flat ladder. That is now what the engine does.
