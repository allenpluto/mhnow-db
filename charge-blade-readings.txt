The Charge Blade on a dummy: what Axe Boost actually does, and what
Power Prolonger and Quick Work are really worth on it
=====================================================================
Three training-dummy runs, 2026-11-18, Thanatos Force 6 (2760 attack,
-30% affinity, no element). The first controlled test in this project:
same weapon, same player, same dummy, one variable changed per run.

   15-26-33   Normal style    no skills
   15-17-57   Axe Boost       no skills
   15-20-53   Axe Boost       Quick Work 3 + Power Prolonger 3

AXE BOOST IS A SELECTED STYLE, NOT A STAT. The weapon page shows it as
node 5 of the style customisation tree, sitting between Attack Power +17
and Attack Power +8 - so it is bought with a node that would otherwise
be raw attack, and the Normal-style run is the same weapon with that
node spent elsewhere.


HOW IT WAS READ
---------------
The dummy's panel gives Total Damage and a HIT count, and it resets to
zero after a few seconds without damage. That reset is the phase
separator: the sword stretch ends at a reset, and the axe stretch counts
up from zero, so the two never have to be separated by arithmetic.

THE BOOST WINDOW IS READ OFF THE ICONS, NOT THE COUNTER. The two weapon
icons beside the phial bar glow red while Axe Boost is up. That matters
more than it sounds: a damage counter cannot tell "the buff expired"
from "the player stopped swinging", and those are the two readings that
decide what Power Prolonger is worth.

   NEAR THE END THE GLOW FLASHES between red and black, which could have
   been mistaken for the window closing. It was not: the detector bridges
   gaps up to 0.5s and the flashes are 1 to 3 frames, while both windows
   end with three or more SOLID seconds of dark. In both runs the player
   was still landing hits after the glow died, so both are expiries.

   The flash is a warning, and a fixed-length one: it begins 10.7s before
   the end in the short window and 9.8s before the end in the long one.


SWORD MODE IS THE SAME IN ALL THREE RUNS
----------------------------------------
   15-26-33  normal       7214 over 10 hits in 6.5s   1110/s
   15-17-57  axe boost    7486 over 10 hits in 6.5s   1152/s
   15-20-53  axe boost    8051 over 11 hits in 7.5s   1073/s

About 1110/s, 1.5 hits/s, 730 per hit, whatever the style and whatever
the skills. That is the control: style and skills do nothing until the
transform, exactly as they should, which is the best evidence that the
method is measuring the weapon and not the recording.


AXE MODE IS WHERE EVERYTHING HAPPENS
------------------------------------
   style       dmg/s    hits/s   per hit
   Normal       2246      1.56      1437
   Axe Boost    3244      5.08       671

   AXE BOOST  +44% damage per second, 3.3x the hit rate, 0.47x per hit

The player's own description - "normal axe makes slow but relatively
high damage attack moves" - is exactly right, and this puts numbers on
it. Axe Boost is not a damage multiplier. It is an ATTACK RATE
multiplier that costs you almost half of each hit, and the rate wins.

THAT DISTINCTION IS WORTH MORE THAN THE 44%. A rate multiplier compounds
with everything priced per hit and does nothing for anything priced per
second; a damage multiplier does the opposite. Nothing in the engine
knows which of the two it is holding, for any weapon.

Against sword mode in the same run: Axe Boost 2.81x, Normal 2.02x.


POWER PROLONGER 3: THE WINDOW DOUBLES, THE DAMAGE DOES NOT
----------------------------------------------------------
   no skills              boost window 32.1s
   Power Prolonger 3                   80.2s      x2.50

A x2.50 window on a completely different mechanic from the Dual Blades
bar this skill was measured on - a discrete buff rather than a drain -
and nothing like the fixed 20%-per-level drain reduction found there.

But the skill is priced in damage, and the cycle arithmetic lands almost
exactly on the number already in the engine. A cycle is the productive
sword stretch (7486 damage over 8.2s, 913/s) plus the transform's dead
time (2.2s) plus the window at 3244/s:

   no skills   8.2 + 2.2 + 32.1 = 42.5s    2625 dmg/s
   Lv3         8.2 + 2.2 + 80.2 = 90.6s    2953 dmg/s    +12.5%

   carried in the engine, extrapolated from Dual Blades:  +12.0%

DOUBLING THE WINDOW BUYS 12% BECAUSE THE WINDOW WAS ALREADY 76% OF THE
CYCLE. This is the same trap Demon Mode sprang: the bar lasted 2.6x
longer at Lv3 and the damage moved 12%. Duration multipliers look
enormous and pay out small whenever the thing being extended already
dominates its cycle.

NOT CHANGED IN THE ENGINE, and now for a good reason rather than a
cautious one - 12.5% measured against 12.0% carried is the closest this
project has come to confirming anything. The cycle model still assumes
the phial recharges from empty every time, which is NOT verified - the
phial bar was part-full during the boost - so the agreement is closer
than the evidence strictly supports. But an extrapolation nobody had
ever tested survived its first contact with a second weapon type, on a
mechanic that shares nothing with the one it was measured on.


QUICK WORK 3: IT PLAINLY WORKS, AND IS STILL PROBABLY OVERPRICED
-----------------------------------------------------------------
FIRST, A CORRECTION THAT WAS THE PLAYER'S CATCH, NOT MINE. This section
originally reported the transform at 1.7s against 1.5s and concluded the
skill did almost nothing. That was an artefact. The Total Damage pill is
SEMI-TRANSPARENT, and the first tick detector watched the whole pill, so
scenery drifting behind it registered as a damage tick and chopped the
front off the gap. Rewritten to watch only the gold digits, and checked
against the player's own frame-by-frame read, the numbers are:

                        button press -> first axe damage    dead time
      no skills                   3.90s                       2.22s
      Quick Work 3                2.05s                       1.13s
                                 -47.4%                      -39.1%

Quick Work 3 cuts the transform roughly in half. That is a large, real,
repeatable effect and the earlier -12% reading was simply wrong.

TWO CLOCKS, BECAUSE THEY ANSWER DIFFERENT QUESTIONS. The transform is
input-buffered: the swing already in progress lands after the button is
pressed, so sword damage keeps arriving for about a second into the
animation. Button-to-damage is what the player feels. Dead time - last
damage of any kind to first axe damage - is the only part a damage model
can actually spend.

THE SMALLER CLOCK IS THE ONE THE ENGINE EATS:

   no skills       cycle 8.2 + 2.22 + 32.1 = 42.5s    2625 dmg/s
   Quick Work 3    cycle 8.2 + 1.13 + 32.1 = 41.4s    2694 dmg/s   +2.6%
   on top of Power Prolonger 3                                     +1.2%

+2.6% against the +15% the engine carries. AND THE BETTER THE BUILD GETS
THE LESS IT IS WORTH: Power Prolonger 3 means one transform a hunt
instead of two or three, so the thing Quick Work speeds up stops
happening. A skill that halves an action can still be worth very little
if the action is 5% of the fight.

AND NORMAL STYLE TRANSFORMS IN HALF THE TIME, which is the third number
and changes the reading of the other two:

   Normal style, no skills      1.87s
   Axe Boost,    no skills      3.90s
   Axe Boost,    Quick Work 3   2.05s

AXE BOOST'S TRANSFORM COSTS TWICE WHAT NORMAL'S DOES. Two seconds of
extra dead time is part of what the style is bought with, alongside the
customisation node. And Quick Work 3 refunds almost exactly that
surcharge - it brings Axe Boost's transform back to Normal's baseline.

That makes +2.6% the skill's BEST case, not its typical one: on Normal
style there is half as much transform to shorten.

CHANGED, 0.05/0.10/0.15 -> 0.011/0.020/0.026, on the same per-level
shape Power Prolonger turned out to have - a fixed ~20% off the
remaining time each level, which reproduces the measured 49% at Lv3.

THE OLD NUMBER WAS NOT MERELY HIGH, IT WAS INVERTING THE RANKING. On the
documented Charge Blade build at 105s, one level of each:

                          before    after
   Quick Work 3           +13.4%    +2.3%
   Critical Ferocity 2    +10.5%   +10.5%
   Lock On 1               +8.9%    +8.9%
   Raw Power 3             +6.8%    +6.8%

The engine was recommending a transform accelerator over every damage
skill on offer. The player reached the opposite conclusion from play -
"consider last base defence i only transformed 3 times, use a damage
boost skill is probably much better" - and the measurement agrees with
them. These are the user's own exchange rates and this is the user's own
revision of them.

IT REMAINS A FLOOR, so if it is wrong it is wrong downward. One action,
on one of the three weapons the skill applies to. WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT:
time a sheathe and a dodge with and without it. If those move, the
number comes back up; if they do not, the transform is the whole skill.


THE NUMBER THAT CHANGES HOW MUCH EVERYTHING ELSE IS WORTH
---------------------------------------------------------
The two Axe Boost runs are different builds, recorded separately. Twenty
seconds into the axe phase they read:

   15-17-57   71,579
   15-20-53   70,579      1.4% apart

A DUMMY RUN IS WORTH ABOUT TWENTY LIVE HUNTS AS EVIDENCE. Live base
defences on one build run +/-33% - two Switch Axe runs minutes apart on
the same set came in at 302,796 and 227,211 the same morning. That
spread has now defeated three build comparisons in this project. The
dummy's is 1.4%.

Everything that failed to settle on live recordings should be re-asked
on a dummy before anyone records another hunt for it.


WHAT THIS STILL CANNOT SEE
--------------------------
   - a dummy never moves, never roars and never knocks you down, so the
     phial-charge half of the cycle is a best case and the boost half
     never gets interrupted
   - whether the phial recharges from empty after a boost expires, which
     the whole cycle model rests on
   - the Switch Axe. Everything above describes what the Charge Blade
     does; none of it describes what the Switch Axe fails to do, and the
     96% gap between them is still unattributed. The same three runs on
     Axe of Demons are the other half and would take twenty minutes.


=====================================================================
THE DUMMY NUMBERS SURVIVE CONTACT WITH A REAL FIGHT
=====================================================================
The red-icon detector was pointed at the three live base defences from
the same morning. This is the test that matters for every dummy figure
above: a dummy never roars, never knocks you down and never moves.

CHARGE BLADE, 12-16-11, fight 9s -> 113.5s:

   Axe Boost  22.7s -> 76.4s   (53.7s)
              82.1s -> 111.8s  (29.7s)

   83.4s boosted out of 104.5s   =  79.8% uptime, two transforms

   dummy cycle model predicted    76% (no Power Prolonger)
                                  88% (Power Prolonger 3)

LIVE UPTIME LANDS BETWEEN THE TWO DUMMY BOUNDS, and the 53.7s first
window is what Power Prolonger 2 should give if each level multiplies
the window by about 1.36 - which is what the build has, since the leg
swap to Bazelgeuse Greaves traded a Power Prolonger slot for Valor 2.
Nothing here was fitted to the live fight; the cycle model was built
entirely on the dummy and the live fight landed inside it.

SWITCH AXE, SAME MORNING, SAME ARMOUR. The Switch Axe's gauge is drawn
as the weapon itself rather than icons and phials, and IT RUNS THE
OPPOSITE WAY ROUND FROM THE CHARGE BLADE:

   grey outline, BLUE bar     axe stage - the default. Attacks CHARGE
                              the bar. Long reach, high per-hit, slow.
   red outline, ORANGE bar    sword stage. Attacks SPEND the bar. Short
                              reach, high frequency, low per-hit, and a
                              charged finisher that explodes and drops
                              you back to axe.

   (Corrected: these were labelled the wrong way round on first write.
   The Charge Blade starts in sword and transforms to axe; the Switch
   Axe starts in axe and transforms to sword.)

BOTH WEAPONS ARE THE SAME CYCLE WITH THE NAMES SWAPPED. A slow state
that builds a resource, and a fast state that spends it:

   Charge Blade    sword charges the phial  ->  AXE BOOST spends it
   Switch Axe      axe charges the bar      ->  SWORD MODE spends it

So the detector was reading each weapon's SPEND state all along, which
is the state worth comparing:

   12-10-30   8 sword windows   17.4 2.1 6.7 8.3 5.2 3.6 19.0 5.0 s
              67.3s of 103.5s   =  65% uptime, EIGHT entries
   12-13-20   6 sword windows in-fight
              63.7s of 102.5s   =  62% uptime, SIX entries

   Charge Blade, same armour:   80% uptime, TWO entries

THIS IS THE PLAYER'S OWN THESIS, MEASURED. The Charge Blade holds its
spend state in two long blocks; the Switch Axe chops it into six or
eight short ones, paying a transform each time and spending a third of
the fight outside it rather than a fifth. The worse of the two Switch
Axe runs has a 26-second stretch - 61s to 88s - with no sword mode at
all, which is the "damage stops growing for a lot of periods" the
player described, visible as a hole in the gauge.

TWO CONSEQUENCES THAT ARE NOT ABOUT THE 96% GAP
-----------------------------------------------
QUICK WORK IS A DIFFERENT SKILL ON EACH WEAPON. It pays per transform,
and these counts are 2 against 6-8. A Charge Blade figure spread to a
Switch Axe would understate it three or four times over - so the
measured 0.011/0.020/0.026 is pinned to the Charge Blade in
utilityTable() and the old flat guess stands elsewhere, the same rule
Evading Reload follows. The engine now says +2.3% on Thanatos Force and
+13.4% on Axe of Demons for the same skill, which is the shape the
evidence actually has.

POWER PROLONGER MAY FIT THE SWITCH AXE BETTER THAN THE CHARGE BLADE.
The Charge Blade's Axe Boost is a timed window; the Switch Axe's sword
stage is a DRAINING BAR, which is precisely the mechanic Power
Prolonger was measured on with Dual Blades' Demon Mode. The 20%-per-
level drain reduction found there has a real chance of transferring
literally rather than by analogy. Untested, and now obviously worth
testing.

WHAT IT STILL DOES NOT ESTABLISH. The Switch Axe's sword stage has
never been put on a dummy, so "fewer spend-state seconds" only matters
if its spend state is worth what the Charge Blade's is. Fragmentation is
now measured on both weapons; the damage rate inside the windows is
measured on one. That is the remaining half of the 96% gap and it is
three dummy runs away.
