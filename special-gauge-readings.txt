Special Gauge and Elemental Release, measured from video
========================================================

Why it matters
--------------
Elemental Release is not a free-floating uptime. The skill reads "elemental
attack power increases by 100% for 20 secs. after activating a Special Skill",
so the uptime is 20 seconds times how many Specials a hunt yields, over its
length. Everything below is about counting Specials.

Method
------
The Special button is tracked frame by frame off recordings resampled to a
constant 20fps, by counting the warm-coloured pixels of the wedge filling it.
Firing the Special empties the button, which shows as a sustained full reading
followed by a fall.

What the HUD actually does, learned the hard way
------------------------------------------------
   - The button is a DIFFERENT SIZE for different weapons, on the same device
     and the same recorder: radius about 21px on an Insect Glaive, about 43px
     on Dual Blades. It has to be located per recording, not assumed.
   - On a training dummy the fill reads yellow; in a real hunt it reads
     orange. One colour test will not do for both.
   - The pre-fight start button is the same colour in nearly the same place,
     which reads as a Special fired before the fight has begun.
   - DUAL BLADES use the same button for two things. One press enters Demon
     Mode; a second press while the gauge is full fires the Special. A red
     swirl on the button means Demon Mode is running and the gauge is NOT
     full, so long red stretches are not a stolen or empty gauge.
   - Also on Dual Blades, the yellow bar under the health bar is Demon Mode's
     own energy, which drains while Demon Mode is up. POWER PROLONGER SLOWS
     THAT DRAIN. That makes Power Prolonger measurable - see the open items.

How fast the gauge fills
------------------------
Two training-dummy runs, attacking without pause:

   Charge Blade,  attack 1450    39.1s and 40.9s between Specials
   Insect Glaive, attack 1155    42.0s and 33.3s

Two weapons 26% apart on attack and TWICE apart on hit rate - 1.2 against 2.5
hits a second - refilled in near enough the same wall-clock time. So the gauge
fills on TIME SPENT ATTACKING, not on damage dealt and not on hits landed.
About 38 seconds of uninterrupted attacking buys one Special.

One real hunt, same Insect Glaive, solo against Rajang: Special fired at 6.9s,
gauge back to 67% by 41.4s, so a full refill would have taken 51.6s. About a
third longer than the dummy, which is the price of not attacking continuously.
The engine uses 52s.

A real hunt end to end
----------------------
Dual Blades, three players, 5-star Riftborne Deviljho, with Valor 3 and
Special Partbreaker 5. The hunt timer read 60 at video t=40, so it began at
t=25 and ran the standard 75 seconds. Specials fired at video t = 34, 47 and
68, which is 9s, 22s and 43s into the hunt.

   windows   9-29, 22-42, 43-63
   merged    9-42, 43-63
   up for    53s of 75s = 70.7%

The engine computes 80% for that hunt - three Specials times 20 seconds over
75 - because it assumes the windows do not overlap. Here the first two were
fired 13 seconds apart, so 7 seconds of the second window fell inside the
first and bought nothing.

So the structure is right and the arithmetic is optimistic by exactly the
overlap. The cause is not the game but the player: firing a Special the moment
the gauge fills wastes Elemental Release when a previous window is still
running. Worth knowing as play advice, and worth not over-fitting a correction
to from one hunt.

Valor and Special Partbreaker both hand over gauge outright rather than
earning it, which is what let three Specials fire in a 75-second hunt when the
natural refill alone would have given two.

What this leaves
----------------
   40s hunt    50%        these are what the engine now uses, against the
   75s hunt    53%        flat 35% that was assumed before any of it was
   150s hunt   40%        measured

With Valor adding its free Special: 100%, 80% and 53%. With Nergigante
Avidity, which stops the gauge filling from attacking at all: 50%, 27% and
13%, or 100%, 53% and 27% if Valor is also carried.

Burst: the limit is activation, not duration   [SUPERSEDED BELOW]
---------------------------------------------
Burst is "+30% attack for 4 seconds" after consecutive hits, and CRUCIALLY the
four seconds RESET on every hit landed while it is up. So once it starts it
runs until a gap longer than four seconds between hits. The four-second figure
is therefore not the duration at all - it is the maximum pause you may take.

That makes the hit record, read off the on-screen HIT counter, the whole
story. Splitting each recording into chains with no gap over four seconds:

   dummy, Insect Glaive   2.21 hits/s   ONE chain, 0.3-81.0   ceiling 100%
   dummy, Charge Blade    1.08 hits/s   3 chains              ceiling  97%
   real fight, Glaive     1.28 hits/s   3 chains              ceiling  99%

So duration never binds. On a glaive hitting twice a second the chain does not
break once in 81 seconds; even the slow Charge Blade only breaks twice in 88.

What binds is ACTIVATION. The Charge Blade's banner - a grey notice panel at
x 0..130, y 264..310 - fired at 41.8s and 51.2s and nowhere else, despite the
player attacking from 1.1s onwards. Taking the first of those as the start,
Burst then rode the hit chain to 81.9s:

   dummy, Charge Blade, Burst 5    up 41.8-81.9 = 40.1s of 88.2s = 45%

Reading 9% earlier was wrong: it counted each banner as a bare four-second
buff and ignored the refresh.

THE GLAIVE RECORDINGS CANNOT BE READ for activation. The banner is a muted
grey panel and in that footage the corner of the screen is dark ground, the
same colour. Counting panel colour gave 50% and 66%, and spot-checking four of
those moments showed scenery every time; requiring the pixels to be stable
frame to frame, since UI does not drift with the camera, then rejected
everything including the genuine banners, because static dark ground is stable
too. Both figures are withdrawn.

So one weapon is measured at 45% against the 35% assumed, and its ceiling is
97%, with the gap being how long Burst took to start. A fast weapon would sit
near its 100% ceiling if it activates at all readily, which is the question
left open.

What would fix it: any dummy recording where the top-left corner holds SKY
rather than ground. The Charge Blade run has it and reads perfectly; it is
purely a matter of where the camera points.

Burst, settled: 50%
-------------------
THE HIT COUNTER CAPS AT 99. Once pegged it stops changing while the fight
carries on, and every chain read off it after that point is wrong - one run
appeared to have a thirteen-second lull in the middle when the player had in
fact never stopped attacking. The Total Damage digits keep climbing and are
the signal to use. All the chains below are read from those.

   Charge Blade dummy, Axe Boost     longest lull 2.55s    ceiling  98%
   Charge Blade dummy, earlier       3 chains              ceiling  97%
   Insect Glaive dummy               one chain, 0.7-81.0   ceiling  99%
   real hunt, Rajang, solo           longest lull 2.55s    ceiling 100%
   real hunt, Deviljho, 3 players    4 lulls over 6s       ceiling  87%

The chain barely ever breaks. A real SOLO hunt held one unbroken chain for its
whole length - the expectation that a moving, attacking monster would shred
Burst's uptime turns out not to hold there, because a hit every four seconds
is a very low bar. Only the three-player hunt broke it, costing an eighth of
the time.

So uptime is set by the FIRST ACTIVATION and nothing else. On the two Charge
Blade runs where the banner is legible the first activation came 41.7s into
one and 12.5s into the other, giving 51% and about 78% of their length.

The engine now carries 50%, the lower of the two, against the 35% that was
assumed. Slow activation is the risk worth respecting, and a fast weapon has
still never been read.

Two detector notes, both earned the hard way. The banner must be found by its
WIDTH - a wide continuous bar of panel colour - not by panel-coloured pixels,
which also catches white sparks against the sky and the grey UI strip at the
left edge. And even that fails where the background is dark ground, which is
every Insect Glaive recording so far; the same false positives at 19s, 33s,
50s and 67s survive both tests and are scenery.

A caveat on the banner itself: it has twice appeared while the buff should
still have been running by the four-second rule, once in each Charge Blade
run. Either it re-announces while active, or the refresh needs hits closer
together than four seconds apart. The user, who was playing, reads the second
case and puts the lapse at a long sword-to-axe transition. The two readings
differ by only a few points on these runs - 74% against 81% - so nothing here
turns on it.

Two flaws in the banner detector, found in live hunts
------------------------------------------------------
THE DETECTOR CANNOT TELL WHICH SKILL FIRED. It finds a pale panel of the
right width and calls it Burst. Three live hunts were decoded and the panels
read "Rude Awakener" and "Special Partbreaker" - every proc announces itself
the same way. Any Burst count that was not confirmed by reading the words is
worthless. The dummy figures above survive because they were read: the
Charge Blade banners at 14.8s, 41.8s, 51.2s and 55.1s all say Burst.

BANNERS STACK, and the crop only saw the first row. In a live Charge Blade
hunt with Burst 2 the first row carried Rude Awakener while BURST SAT IN THE
SECOND ROW at y 325 to 371, invisible to a crop that stopped at y 310. The
first pass therefore reported two Burst activations that were both Rude
Awakener, and missed the one real Burst entirely.

The banner shows only the top TWO skills, so a Burst firing while two others
are announcing would not appear at all. That did NOT bite in the hunt below:
the set carries Status Sneak Attack, Rude Awakener, Raw Power, Burst, Health
Boost, Evade Extender, Artful Dodger, Lock On and Power Prolonger, but only
Rude Awakener and Burst announce, so two announcers never contend for two
slots. The count that follows is a count, not a floor. It is a trap to keep
in mind on a set with three or more announcing skills.

THE DUMMY RUNS ARE SAFE FROM THIS, which was worth checking rather than
assuming. The second banner row is empty for the whole of both Charge Blade
dummy recordings - no sustained panel in 88s or 67s - so nothing ever
competed with Burst for a slot. That follows: nothing attacks you on a dummy,
so the skills that would compete never fire. The 50% the engine carries rests
on those runs and stands.

Widening the crop to both rows and reading it: in 37 seconds of a live hunt
on a fast weapon, BURST ACTIVATED ONCE, at about 20.5s.

BURST IN A LIVE HUNT: 27%
--------------------------
A live hunt has no Total Damage counter, which is what blocked this for so
long. It does have the MONSTER'S HEALTH BAR, and that shortens every time
damage lands - so in a SOLO hunt the bar is the hit record. Reading the
purple fill of the bar at x 105..385, y 172..190 and taking every new low as
a hit:

   Charge Blade, solo against Riftborne Pukei-Pukei, 37s
     39 hits, chains 8.4-16.9, 20.1-30.5, 30.6-34.5
     longest lulls 7.15s at 12.9s and 4.05s at 26.5s
     Burst activated 20.5s and rode the chain to 30.5s
     UP FOR 10.0s OF 37.0s = 27%

Against 51% and 81% on the dummy. The user predicted exactly this - a monster
that moves out of range and interrupts breaks the chain where a dummy never
does - and here is the first measurement of it.

Two cautions. The bar is about 265 pixels for the monster's whole health, so
a small hit may not move it a full pixel and would read as a lull that was
not one; and the 4.05s lull at 26.5s sits right on the four-second threshold,
so it alone decides whether Burst stopped at 30.5s or ran on. One clip of 37
seconds on one weapon is not enough to move the engine off 50% by itself.

Only SOLO hunts work for this. In a group the bar falls to everyone's hits,
so it stops being a record of yours. That is the same order
as the dummy runs, which first activated at 12.5s and 41.7s, so nothing
contradicts the 50% the engine carries - but the live hit chain cannot be
read, there being no damage counter in a hunt, so the uptime that follows
from that activation is not measured either.

Open items
----------
   - POWER PROLONGER is still a flat 5/10/15% guess across seven weapon
     types. On Dual Blades it is now known to slow the Demon Mode energy
     drain, and that bar is on screen under the health bar. Timing Demon Mode
     from entry to collapse at Power Prolonger 0 and again at 3 would turn
     that guess into a measurement, on the weapon where the skill matters
     most.
   - BURST is still a flat 35% guess away from the Charge Blade's 45%. What
     is missing is how readily it ACTIVATES on a fast weapon, since duration
     never binds. A dummy run on a fast weapon, camera pointed so the
     top-left corner holds sky, answers it in one recording.
   - DEMON MODE on Dual Blades needs its damage measured, not just its
     length. The dummy's Total Damage counter gives it exactly: attack
     continuously in normal stance for a stretch, then through a whole Demon
     Mode until it drops by itself, and the counter at each boundary gives
     damage per second in both states. The red swirl on the Special button
     marks the Demon Mode window automatically. Done twice - once without
     Power Prolonger and once at max - it prices the skill properly: extra
     seconds of Demon Mode times the gap in damage per second.
   - Specials fired close together waste uptime, and the engine does not
     model it. One hunt is not enough to say how much is typically wasted.
