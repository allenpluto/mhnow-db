Dual Blades: Demon Mode, styles, and what Power Prolonger buys
==============================================================

Method
------
Three training-dummy recordings, one per style, decoded at 20fps. The energy
bar under the health bar (x 150..332, y 944..958) is the state signal: it
drains while Demon Mode runs and refills while it does not. It is YELLOW
normally and RED under Feral Demon Mode, which is how the two are told apart -
a yellow-only test reads Feral as an empty bar. Damage comes from the dummy's
own Total Damage counter, read at each boundary.

The style is named in the dropdown at the top of the screen, so each recording
says which style it is without being told.

Energy, by style
----------------
   No style      drains 5.2%/s   full bar lasts 19.2s
                 refills 5.6%/s  back to full in 17.9s
   Feral Demon   drains 13.5%/s  full bar lasts 7.4s
                 refills ~6.4%/s
   Spiral Slash  drains ~5.5%/s, plus the Spiral Slash itself eats a large
                 chunk outright - the bar fell 74% to 0 in 1.5s during one

So Feral Demon burns energy 2.6 times faster than plain Demon Mode. That is
the trade the style is named for.

Damage per second, measured
---------------------------
   style          state              dmg/s   hits/s   dmg/hit
   No style       demon, standing     1732     3.26      557
   No style       demon, moving       1216     4.22      288
   No style       normal               985     2.53      389
   Feral Demon    feral demon         1369     2.62      523
   Feral Demon    normal              1912     3.33      574
   Spiral Slash   demon, standing     1392     3.19      436
   Spiral Slash   the Spiral Slash    1753     2.60      672
   Spiral Slash   normal               896     2.34      383

THE SEGMENT AVERAGES ABOVE MISLEAD ON TWO ROWS. Sampling the damage counter
every 1.5s through the first nine seconds of each run shows why:

   seconds     No style demon    Feral demon     Spiral Slash style
   0.3 - 1.5        1338 dmg/s       554 dmg/s         947 dmg/s
   1.5 - 3.0        1811            1071              1551
   3.0 - 4.5        2639             887              1095
   4.5 - 6.0         984            1559               960
   6.0 - 7.5        2016            2159              3096   <- airborne
   7.5 - 9.0        1871            1752              3383   <- airborne

Feral RAMPS. Its first three seconds run at a third of its later rate, because
the measured window contains the entry into Feral Demon Mode while No style's
window does not - that recording was already in Demon Mode at frame zero. Take
the ramp out and compare like with like, 4.5s to 9.0s in both:

   No style demon   1624 dmg/s
   Feral demon      1823 dmg/s

So Feral is NOT weaker once it is running; it is 12% stronger, which is what
the style promises. Its problem is that a roughly three-second entry eats a
large share of a window that only lasts 7.4 seconds.

And the Spiral Slash itself is far better than the segment average suggested.
Across 6.0s to 9.0s, the airborne part, it runs at 3239 dmg/s - nearly double
Demon Mode standing and about three and a half times normal stance.

THE TWO SPIRAL SLASHES, SEPARATELY. The hit record isolates them cleanly: the
airborne spin is far denser than anything on the ground, 7 or 8 hits a second
against 2 or 3.

   first slash    airborne hits 6.75s - 8.20s    1.45s, 12 hits, 8422 dmg
   second slash   airborne hits 20.85s - 21.60s  0.75s,  7 hits, 5362 dmg

So the second really is about HALF as long in the air, which is the red mark
at the centre of the energy bar having been spent on the first. The first
slash also collapsed the bar outright - 74% to 0 between 5.4s and 6.9s - while
the second drained it at the ordinary 6.7%/s and took nothing extra.

But it is not weaker while it lasts. Per hit it is slightly HARDER, 766
against 702. Counting each whole cycle from the last attack on the ground to
half a second past the last hit in the air:

   first slash    6.10s - 8.70s   2.60s   8422 dmg   3239 dmg/s
   second slash   20.40s - 22.10s 1.70s   5362 dmg   3154 dmg/s

Within a rounding error of each other. The red mark buys DURATION, not power:
the second slash delivers 36% less damage because it is 35% shorter, not
because it hits any softer.

The landing moment is the soft part of this - the counter only shows that no
damage lands after it, not the exact frame of touchdown - so the half second
allowed for the descent is the same for both rather than measured for each.

Neither effect is a big opening hit, and neither is the recorder stuttering.
No style's FIRST interval is its slowest of the six, so a large first hit is
not inflating it; and a stutter would show as a flat stretch, where Feral
shows a steady climb.

Three further findings fall out.

DEMON MODE IS WORTH 1.76x NORMAL on No style, standing still. It gets there
from both directions: 43% more damage a hit and 29% more hits a second.

ATTACKING WHILE MOVING COSTS A THIRD. Demon Mode's moving attack lands MORE
often - 4.22 hits a second against 3.26 - but each hit is worth half as much,
288 against 557, so it nets 1216 against 1732. Movement is bought with damage,
not given free.

FERAL DEMON MODE LOSES TO ITS OWN NORMAL STANCE over a whole window, 1369
against 1912 - which the user predicted from feel. But the cause is the entry
ramp above, not weak attacks: running, Feral beats No style's Demon Mode. The
style is a short expensive burst with a long start, and the start is most of
why it does not pay.

The Feral style's NORMAL stance at 1912 is the single number here most worth
re-checking, since the other two styles agree with each other on normal stance
- 985 and 896 - and only this one is nearly double.

What that makes of the whole cycle
----------------------------------
Attacking continuously and using Demon Mode whenever the bar allows:

   No style      demon up 19.2 / (19.2 + 17.9) = 52% of the time
                 cycle averages 1372 dmg/s, against 985 never using it  +39%
   Feral Demon   demon up 7.4 / (7.4 + 15.6) = 32%
                 cycle averages 1738 dmg/s, against 1912 NEVER USING IT

So on Feral Demon the best play looks like never entering Demon Mode at all.
That rests on the 1912 figure, so it rests on re-checking it.

Power Prolonger: MEASURED
-------------------------
Three further runs, styles confirmed from the dropdown rather than assumed:

   No style       Lv0   5.23%/s   bar lasts 19.1s
                  Lv2   3.13%/s             31.9s    1.67x
                  Lv3   2.00%/s             50.0s    2.62x
   Feral Demon    Lv0   13.5%/s              7.4s
                  Lv3   5.05%/s             19.8s    2.67x

THE RULE IS A FIXED REDUCTION PER LEVEL: 20% of the base drain each. Lv2
implies 20.1% a level and Lv3 implies 20.6%, so drain = base x (1 - 0.2 x
level) and Lv3 leaves two fifths of it. Independently, that same 20% predicts
Feral's window stretching 2.50x where it actually stretched 2.67x.

Note what is linear and what is not. The DRAIN falls in equal steps; the
DURATION does not, because it is one over the drain - 1.00x, 1.25x, 1.67x,
2.50x. Reading the ladder off the duration would have looked wildly
non-linear and been wrong.

Turning it into damage needs the whole cycle. Demon Mode is worth 1732 dmg/s
against normal stance's 985, and the bar refills at 5.6%/s, so a cycle is the
bar's life plus 17.9 seconds of refilling:

   Lv0   demon up 52% of the cycle   1371 dmg/s   (baseline)
   Lv1   up 57%                      1413         +3.0%   drain interpolated
   Lv2   up 64%                      1464         +6.8%
   Lv3   up 74%                      1535        +12.0%

Against the 5/10/15% assumed. Lower, but the same shape, and Lv3 at 12% is
close enough to the user's 15% that the estimate from play was a good one.

The engine now uses this ladder on ALL SEVEN weapon types the skill names, at
the user's direction, not only on Dual Blades. The mechanic it slows differs
on each - a spirit gauge, a phial charge, a song - so that is an
extrapolation, and the better of the two options on offer: a number measured
on one weapon beats one measured on none. If another type is ever timed it
should get its own entry rather than displacing this.

A DUMMY CANNOT SEE ALL OF IT. Demon Mode's other advantage is attacking
through a slide while a monster is swinging at you, and nothing here measures
that. These figures are a floor.

The prediction that it would be worth MORE on Feral holds, for the reason
given earlier: Feral pays a fixed three-second entry out of a window that was
only 7.4s long. At Lv3 that window becomes 19.8s, so the entry falls from 40%
of it to 15%, and the style's own damage per second rises as well as its
length. Pricing that properly needs the 1912 figure for Feral's normal stance
re-checked first, since it is what decides whether Feral's Demon Mode is worth
entering at all.

Feral's normal stance: confirmed, and it is the best thing on the weapon
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A dedicated run, Feral Demon Mode style, normal stance only, attacking without
pause. The counter is almost perfectly linear:

    6.0s   8706 dmg   17 hits
   11.0s  18458       34
   16.0s  28210       51
   21.0s  37962       68

Exactly 9752 damage and 17 hits in every five seconds. 1950 dmg/s, 3.4 hits a
second, 574 a hit. The earlier 1912 was right.

So the Feral style's NORMAL stance out-damages everything else measured on
this weapon:

   Feral style, normal stance     1950 dmg/s
   Feral style, demon (running)   1823
   No style, demon, standing      1732
   No style, demon, moving        1216
   No style, normal                985
   Spiral style, normal            896

Against No style's normal at 985, Feral's normal does double - from both
directions, 574 a hit against 389 and 3.40 hits a second against 2.53.

The consequence is awkward and worth stating plainly: on the Feral style the
best sustained damage is to NEVER ENTER DEMON MODE. Cycling it gives 1777
dmg/s, and 1792 even with Power Prolonger 3 stretching the window, against
1950 for simply not bothering. Power Prolonger is worth nothing on a style
whose Demon Mode you should not use.

That is a dummy result. Demon Mode's other half is attacking through a slide
while a monster swings at you, which no dummy can show.

The red dot, measured clean
---------------------------
With Burst 5 taken off the set, so nothing intermittent is riding on the
numbers:

   with the dot      20 hits   9968 dmg   498 a hit
   without the dot   15 hits   7613 dmg   508 a hit

The dot is worth FIVE MORE HITS, a third again, at identical power - 498
against 508 is noise. It buys duration and nothing else, which is what the
first pair of slashes suggested and this now says cleanly.

NO BURST UPTIME CAN BE READ OFF THIS. An earlier draft divided the same slash
with Burst 5, 11560, by the figure without it, 9968, and called the resulting
1.16 on a +30% skill "Burst up for half the move". That is wrong. Burst does
not cover the Burst-5 runs evenly - it came up partway through one move and
was already running at the start of another, since a slash taken straight
after a previous one inherits a buff that is still live. A ratio between two
moves with different Burst coverage measures the difference in coverage, not
the skill.

Which is exactly why the runs above were redone with the skill off the set,
and why only those numbers are used here.

A WRONG READING CORRECTED. An earlier draft of this file took three groups of
exactly fifteen hits in the Power Prolonger 3 run - 8500 damage, 8500 again,
then 9898 - to be without-the-dot slashes. They are MOVING ATTACKS. The user
flagged the first and the energy bar confirms all three: a slash eats a
visible chunk of the bar, and after the one at 7s the bar drains at a flat
2.29%/s, its passive rate, from 14s all the way to empty at 39s, leaving no
room for another slash's cost. The fifteen-hit count is a coincidence between
the two moves, and they differ on power once Burst is removed.

That the bar is the only reliable test also puts a question over the earlier
pair of slashes timed from hit density alone. The first of those did collapse
the bar, 74% to 0; the second did not, so it may have been a moving attack
too. Density is not enough on its own - a moving attack lands about 3.75 hits
a second and an airborne slash 5 to 8, and those overlap.

Power Prolonger cuts the slash's cost, not just the drain
----------------------------------------------------------
Taking the passive drain out of each run leaves what the slash itself ate:

   Power Prolonger 0   bar 94% to 1% over 4.5s, passive would be 25%
                       -> the slash cost about 68% of the bar
   Power Prolonger 3   bar 100% to 55% over 7.0s, passive would be 16%
                       -> the slash cost about 29%

A ratio of 0.43 where the passive drain's own ratio at Lv3 is 0.40. So Power
Prolonger appears to cut the slash's cost by about the same factor as it cuts
the drain, which would allow roughly three slashes a bar at Lv3 against one
and a half at Lv0 - what the user predicted before recording it.

PREDICTED, NOT OBSERVED. Only one slash was actually performed in the Lv3
run, so the three-slashes figure is arithmetic from the cost, not something
seen. A run that simply slashes as often as the bar allows would settle it.

In the engine
-------------
Dual Blades now choose a style the way bowguns do: three variants, each
searched as its own build, with the best winning. The rates are relative to
No style, since that is what the damage model calls one.

   no style                                      1.00   PP 3.0 / 6.8 / 12.0%
   Feral Demon Mode, played in normal stance     1.42   PP    0 /   0 /    0
   Spiral Slash                                  0.86   PP 2.9 / 6.0 / 15.3%

Power Prolonger is no longer one number on this weapon. It lengthens Demon
Mode, so on No style it is worth the measured ladder; on Feral it is worth
nothing, because the Demon Mode it lengthens should not be entered; and on
Spiral Slash it is worth more than either, because it cuts the slash's own
bar cost as well as the passive drain and so buys extra slashes. The Spiral
figures are derived from those two measurements rather than timed.

A related bug fixed while doing it. The search collapses two styles that
reach the same skill totals, and used to label every such pair "either style
reaches this set". That was true when all styles scored alike; it stopped
being true once styles carried their own damage rates, and the note was being
printed on pairs that differ by 40%. It now only appears when the scores
actually tie.

Twenty of the fifty-five Dual Blades in the data are style selectable, so the
other thirty-five are unaffected.

A CAVEAT ON THE WEAPON. The screenshot sent with the three dummy runs is
Bloom Snow Wail+, which is a CHARGE BLADE, so which Dual Blades those runs
used is not actually recorded. Nothing above depends on it - every figure is
a measured damage rate and every comparison is between styles on the same
weapon within the same session - but the absolute dmg/s cannot be carried to
another weapon without knowing the attack behind them.

What to record next
-------------------
Nothing urgent. If the Spiral Slash figure is ever worth pinning, a Lv1 and
Lv2 run would turn that single 0.35 into a ladder and say whether the slash
cost falls with the same 20% a level as the passive drain does.
